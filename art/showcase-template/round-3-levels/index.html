LEVELS 1 → 20

Kitchen Sink (Showcase Template) · game-systems proposal · 2026-11-02 · plan docs/plans/KITCHEN-SINK.md

You walk into the kitchen sink at level 1. Everything east of the main road wants to kill you; everything west of it is safe and pays nothing. You level by killing robots and crates, and only by killing them. Six enemy types sit in six level bands, three mini-bosses stand at 6, 12 and 18, and the Crate Titan waits at 20. This page is the research behind that, the full rule set with numbers, and the engine work it needs.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • 8 attributes, shared by players and enemies: Vitality, Strength, Dexterity, Precision, Agility, Toughness, Resolve, Poise.
  • Hybrid allocation: each level adds +1 to all eight by itself, and you place 3 points where you like (3 more at character creation). Cap 40 per attribute. Free respec at the hub.
  • XP only from kills. 34,200 XP to reach 20; about 2.5–3 hours of grinding. Equal-level kills pay 20 + 5 × level. Enemies below you pay less and pay nothing at 6 levels below (Guild Wars 1's rule).
  • Hits always land when the blade connects. Stats decide how well: a glance (half damage), a clean hit, or a crit (×1.5). Being under-levelled means more glances, less damage, thicker armour.
  • A fight with an equal-level enemy takes 6–8 sword swings, about 8–12 seconds on the phone. A mini-boss takes about a minute. The Titan takes 3–4 minutes.
  • Bosses are a soft gate: always open, and the level-gap maths makes them brutal if you come early.

What we take

  • Guild Wars 1: cap 20, kill-XP table that hits 0 at six levels under, refundable points.
  • WoW: con colours on the nameplate, glancing blows on higher foes, armour that weakens against higher attackers.
  • Fire Emblem / Pokémon: one stat block for both sides, so an enemy is a character at a level.
  • Ragnarok / Diablo II: level sits inside hit maths on both sides; chosen points with weapon requirements.
  • Elden Ring: eight attributes is enough for real builds when weapons scale off them.

2 · THE 8 ATTRIBUTES

Every actor, player or robot, has a level and the same eight numbers. Everyone starts each attribute at level + 4 (5 at level 1). Damage comes in two types: physical (blades, bolts, bullets, fists, crates) and energy (burn, shock, blast: pitch bolts, welding torches, arc lances).

VIT · VitalityMax HP: 40 + 12 × VIT + 10 × (level − 1). Out-of-combat regen 4 % of max HP a second.
STR · StrengthSword and spear damage: +4 % per point over 5. More stagger dealt.
DEX · DexterityBow, crossbow and rifle damage: +4 % per point over 5. Faster draw and reload (−1 % a point).
PRE · PrecisionCrit chance 5 % + 0.8 % per point over 5. Each point cuts the target's glance chance by 1.2 %.
AGI · AgilityEach point adds 1.2 % glance chance to hits on you. Faster dodge recovery (−1 % a point).
TGH · ToughnessPhysical armour: rating 4 × TGH, cuts physical damage (formula below), max 75 %.
RES · ResolveEnergy armour: rating 4 × RES against burn, shock and blast; shorter burn and shock.
POI · PoiseStagger bar 20 + 4 × POI. Sword parry window 0.15 s + 0.005 s a point over 5 (max 0.33 s).

Why these eight: four for offence (STR, DEX, PRE and the weapon choice they imply), four for defence (VIT, AGI, TGH, RES), and Poise for the parry and stagger game the sword and the big robots need. Every enemy type leans on two or three of them, so it reads as a character, not a bag of HP.

Allocation: hybrid RECOMMENDED

  • Automatic: every level adds +1 to all eight. Nobody can build a broken character, and enemies grow on the same floor.
  • Chosen: 3 points per level (and 3 at creation), cap 40 per attribute. At 20 that is 60 free points: enough to max three attributes, so builds are real.
  • Free respec at the hub's Tuning Bench (GW1's rule), so trying the bow after the sword costs nothing.
  • Presets for phone players who want one tap: Blade (STR, VIT, PRE), Marksman (DEX, PRE, AGI), Bulwark (VIT, TGH, POI).
  • A level-up refills HP, plays the sting, shows "LEVEL 7" and puts a "+3" badge on the pause button.

Fully automatic (WoW, FE) would leave the attributes as decoration; fully chosen (Diablo II) lets a new player dump everything into Strength and die at level 9 with 300 HP. Hybrid keeps the floor and the choice.

4 · COMBAT MATHS

Contact decides the hit; stats decide its quality. On a phone action game a blade that visibly connects and shows "MISS" feels broken, so there is no miss roll. A connected hit is a glance (half damage, grey number), a clean hit (white) or a crit (×1.5, orange, bigger). Real misses come from your aim and their dodges.

damage = max(1, round( B × S × M × G × (1 − A) × Q )) B base weapon tier base (player) · 12 × (1 + 0.1 × (L − 1)) × strike multiplier (enemy) S scaling 1 + 0.04 × (STR or DEX − 5) the weapon's attribute M move combo 1 / 1 / 1.33 · heavy 2 · headshot 1.5 (rifle 2) · ammo row G gap g = defender level − attacker level g > 0: 1 − 0.07 g (min 0.5) g < 0: 1 + 0.04 |g| (max 1.2) A armour R / (R + 60 + 8 × attacker level), max 0.75 R = 4 × TGH (physical) or 4 × RES (energy) Q quality glance 0.5 · clean 1 · crit 1.5 glance % = 8 + 1.2 × (AGI def − PRE att) + 6 × max(0, g) − 3 × max(0, −g) [0–60] crit % = 5 + 0.8 × (PRE att − 5) − 2 × max(0, g) [0–50] a glance never crits; crits roll on clean hits only

The level gap counts three times when you are under-levelled: G cuts your damage 7 % a level, the armour term gets stronger because your level is in its denominator, and glances climb 6 % a level. The enemy, a character at a higher level, also simply has more HP and hits harder. Over-levelled, it all runs the other way, but gently.

Worked example

You: level 10 Blade (STR 24, PRE 24), Steel sword (tier III, base 22), third combo swing. It: level 12 Welder Unit (TGH 16, AGI 16, 255 HP). Gap g = +2.

B 22 × S (1 + 0.04 × 19 = 1.76) × M 1.33 = 51.5 G 1 − 0.07 × 2 = 0.86 → 44.3 A R = 64; 64 / (64 + 60 + 80) = 0.31 → × 0.69 = 30.4 clean 30 · crit 45 · glance 15 glance 8 + 1.2 × (16 − 24) + 12 = 10.4 % · crit 5 + 15.2 − 4 = 16.2 % → about 8 swings for its 255 HP

10 · DEATH

  • You respawn at the last discovered place (the game's LastPlace, already wired for every shard), full HP.
  • No XP loss, no item loss, no coin loss. XP is the one currency you grind; taking it away on a phone feels awful.
  • You get Morale −10 % (max HP and damage), stacking to −30 % (Guild Wars 1's death penalty, softened). Five non-grey kills remove one stack; the hub clears all of it.
  • The enemy that killed you heals to full; a mini-boss or the Titan resets its phase. The "Killed by…" card names it.

11 · ENGINE WORK (WAVE 1)

What exists today: one ordered damage pipeline (CombatPipeline, damage.modify stages with cap / add / mul rules, damageTakenMul, incomingCap), an EffectService whose effects already modify named attributes, flat damage numbers on weapon profiles, creature strikes and boss modes, boss hp in boss profiles, the player fixed at 100 HP with 4 HP/s regen, coin Bounty and LastPlace. There is no level, no XP, no attribute and no crit anywhere; sword parry exists only in the legacy Nine Dragon shard.

The new rows and hosts. Every one goes through the host bar with Kitchen Sink as a real user; the shard writes data and AssemblyScript only.

  1. Progression rows (src/format/progression.ts): the 8 attribute ids, auto growth, points per level, the cap, the XP table, the kill-XP and gap tables, ranks, and every coefficient on this page. Game-layer defaults; a shard opts in.
  2. Actor level and attributes (engine combat): level plus the eight on the actor's existing AttributeSet, so effects can buff STR or drop TGH (the Titan's open core) with no new code.
  3. The stat stage in CombatPipeline: one damage.modify answer that applies S, G, A and Q; DamageRequest gains damageType, scaling and an outcome (glance / clean / crit) for the numbers. incomingCap is off on levelled shards (it would flatten the gap).
  4. Player health: max HP from VIT and level, regen as 4 % of max, full heal on level-up.
  5. Experience host (game layer): pays XP on actor.died with the drop-off, raises the level, saves it with the character (one character across shards), feeds achievements ("Level 10", "Level 20").
  6. Creature and spawn rows: level or band, lean weights and rank; strike damage becomes a strike multiplier on the level base; 90 s respawn in combat zones; night-shift variants.
