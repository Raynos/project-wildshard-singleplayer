COMBAT MATHS: THREE DAMAGE FORMULAS

Kitchen Sink v2 · levels-combat §13 · 2026-11-03 · seven real MMOs, three families, one calculator

THE SHORT ANSWER

Jake asked: "What are the best 3 options available? I think raw addition and subtraction might be a bad idea." and then "look at real MMORPG designs … as you get stronger, number goes up, numbers get bigger."

  • You're right about subtraction. Fire Emblem's ATK − DEF works for 2–4 blows a fight. With dozens of hits a fight and seven rolled attributes, it makes walls (1-damage hits), lets the rifle out-damage the sword 2:1 early, and loses elite fights to the dice. It stays on this page only as the control.
  • The three best, all from real MMOs: WoW-classic style (armour takes a share, scaled by the attacker's level), GW1 style (damage doubles or halves per 40 points of strike-minus-armour), GW2 / Lineage 2 style (attack ÷ armour).
  • Recommended: WoW-classic style. Your sword hits for 14 at level 1 and 137 at level 20; a common has 88 HP at level 1 and 849 at level 20; the Titan has 18,700. It hits every target in the brief and is the only family with no breaks: no walls, no one-shots, DEF-heavy foes stay fair.
  • A tension you need to settle: your hits grow about 10× from level 1 to 20, but VIT is locked at 100 → 300 (3×). So enemies would have to hit you for 10 at level 1 and only 30 at level 20 while you hit for 137. Recommended: VIT 100 → 600 (6×, what WoW's levels 1–20 do), so their numbers go up too. Q2 below.

Each family's enemy HP curve and enemy strike size are fitted so an average character at equal level kills a common in about 7 sword swings and dies to about 11 of its strikes. THE TENSION: 10× DAMAGE, 3× HP

Your hit grows about 10× from level 1 to 20 (STR 6× and Forge 1.6×). Common HP grows with it (88 → 849): enemies' HP curves are steeper, as ruled. But your own HP is VIT, locked at 100 → 300. For an equal-level enemy to still need ~11 strikes, its strike must grow only 3× while its STR grows 6×, so an enemy level factor has to shrink its strikes about 3.5 % a level (×0.96). It works, but the two sides stop following the same rules, and an enemy hitting you for 30 while you hit it for 137 reads as "they're weak".

  • A. Keep ×3 (100 → 300): enemies hit 10 → 30, with a hidden shrink factor. Lowest numbers on you.
  • B. ×6 (100 → 600, +9.9 % a level, cap 660): VIT grows like STR and DEX, so the enemy's strike needs no fudge (fitted factor 1.00). Enemies hit 10 → 60. WoW's 1–20 does about this.
  • C. ×10 (100 → 1,000): big numbers both ways (10 → 99), but now enemies need a growing fudge (×1.03 a level).

SEVEN GROWTH SHAPES

Every attribute grows by Jake's rule: each level adds round(average step × aptitude × swing), aptitude two 1.2, three 1.0, two 0.8, swing 0.4–1.6, cap ~10 % over the level-20 average. What differs is each one's average curve. The chart shows each as a share of its level-20 average so the shapes compare.

Enemies use the same curves × their kind's leans (no swing): Riveter DEF ×1.8, Plumb Drone DEF ×0.6 and HP ×0.6, Welder MDEF ×1.5, and so on (calculator).

WEAPONS AND FORGE

Animation and attack speed are fixed (no speed from AGI). "Cadence" is one attempt per that many seconds in a real fight, footwork included: the TTK tables use it. Melee trades safety for about 10 % more damage a second.

Forge I–IV: ×1.0 / ×1.2 / ×1.4 / ×1.6 on every weapon (one-off buys at levels 1, 6, 11, 16). Each buy cuts swings per kill by about 15 %; the HP curves are fitted to the smooth average, so a fresh Forge feels strong and wears off over five levels (7.9 swings just before a buy, 6.7 after).
