ATTRIBUTES: 20 GAMES AND OUR FOUR

Kitchen Sink v2 · levels and combat · 2026-11-03 · draft for Jake · replaces the old eight (round 3)

SHORT VERSION

Four attributes, the shape you described: VIT is your HP. STR is melee damage. DEX is ranged damage, your chance to land a hit with every weapon, and melee crits. AGI is dodging and ranged crits.

No armour attribute. Armour is your level (the level gap already does most of the defending, as in Ragnarok, Diablo II and Guild Wars 1) times a plating number on each robot's body (Monster Hunter's hard parts, as data). The player's plating is 1.0, and the Warden's Plate and Arc Insulation upgrades stay.

Aptitude with four: one 1.2, two 1.0, one 0.8 per shard: your eight-attribute rule (two, four, two) halved. Each shard rolls you one strong and one weak attribute. That gives 12 different characters, each of them balanced.

The research below supports it: strength and health appear in almost every game, accuracy usually rides DEX, games that made hit chance its own stat later removed it, and action games keep defence on gear or level, not as a stat.

ATTRIBUTES THAT SHOW UP MOST

How many of the 20 have each kind of attribute (a game counts once per kind). Orange bars are the jobs our four cover.

What the 20 teach

  • Strength (19 of 20) and health (16) are universal. Nobody needs VIT or STR explained.
  • Most games split skill from speed: a DEX-like stat (skill, aim, ranged) and an AGI-like stat (speed, evasion) are separate in WoW, EverQuest, Ragnarok, Fire Emblem (Skill and Speed) and Path of Exile.
  • Accuracy is a stat in 10 games, but its own stat in only 3 (RuneScape's Attack, Black Desert's Accuracy, WoW's hit rating). In 6 it rides DEX or Skill (Ragnarok, Diablo II, Path of Exile, D&D, Fire Emblem, Morrowind's Agility). The games that had a separate accuracy chase cut it: WoW in 2014, FFXIV in 2017, Elder Scrolls after Morrowind. Your "DEX is hit chance" is the common shape.
  • Crit sits on the finesse stat: Fire Emblem's Skill (hit and crit, exactly your DEX), WoW Classic's Agility (crit and dodge, exactly your AGI), Ragnarok's Luck.
  • Dead stats are the most common complaint: Dark Souls 1's Resistance, Lost Ark's three "trap stats", Charisma in EverQuest, Luck in Ragnarok. Our growth is automatic, so nobody wastes points, but an attribute with no visible effect is still noise. Each of our four shows up on screen in every fight.
  • Balanced random growth already exists: Pokémon's natures give +10 % to one stat and −10 % to another. Our aptitude roll works the same way, across the whole sheet.
  • Growth tied to a choice breeds regret: Dragon's Dogma players level as one vocation and then switch to farm better stats. Your automatic per-shard roll avoids that by design.

Defence without gear armour

The Kitchen Sink has no armour drops: fixed rewards, Forge I–IV for weapons. The games that also avoid gear armour handle defence in four ways:

  • Defence is an attribute that grows with level: Fire Emblem (Def), Pokémon (Defense), Dragon's Dogma. All three are turn-based or close to it, where the stat sheet is the whole game.
  • The level is the defence: Ragnarok puts the level in both HIT and FLEE, Diablo II scales hit chance by both levels, and Guild Wars 1 monsters get their armour from their level.
  • A short, capped ladder bought once: Guild Wars 1's max armour is cheap and the same for everyone at 20. That is our Forge, and it already exists for weapons.
  • Hard parts on the body: Monster Hunter puts the toughness in the monster's hitzones (data), and blades bounce off.

The recommendation takes the second and the fourth: your level is your armour, and each robot's body carries a plating number. That keeps the DEFLECT clang meaningful: a Riveter clangs more than a Crate Mite at the same level.

2 · THREE OPTIONS

All three keep the locked rules: VIT is the HP (100 → 300, cap 330), the failure roll is 12 % at equal level and 90 % at +8 with no streak guard, failures show as DEFLECT, DODGE or PARRY, and enemies use the same attributes. Every attribute follows one par curve, the average path P(L) = 100 × 3^((L−1)/19) (100 at L1, 168 at L10, 300 at L20), and grows by the locked model (par step × aptitude × swing, cap 330).

The rule that keeps the level gap harsh: attributes move the failure roll by your edge: how far a value sits above or below par for its owner's level. Level-driven growth never counts twice. Each 1 % of edge moves the roll by 0.5 points, capped at ±8, so the best roll is worth less than one level (9.75). Edges at level 10 run from about 0.92 (aptitude 0.8) to 1.08 (1.2).

The aptitude spread

  • Four attributes: one 1.2, two 1.0, one 0.8, shuffled per shard: the eight-attribute rule (two, four, two) halved, so a quarter strong, a quarter weak and the total neutral. Twelve characters: Tank (VIT 1.2), Brawler (STR), Marksman (DEX), Dancer (AGI), each paired with one of three weak spots.
  • Five (option B): one 1.2, three 1.0, one 0.8. Six or seven: still one 1.2 and one 0.8, the rest 1.0. Eight: two, four, two as ruled.
  • VIT is strong in a quarter of shards and weak in a quarter, exactly as with eight. The VIT numbers from round 8 are unchanged.
  • Where the roll lands (20,000 simulated characters): aptitude 1.2 averages edge 1.08 at L10 and 1.10 at L20 (cap 330); 0.8 averages 0.92 and 0.87. The edge is what moves the roll, so a strong DEX at level 10 takes about 4 points off your failures and adds about 4 to your melee crit.

Why not a fifth (Defence)?

  • The level gap already defends: 9.75 points a level on the roll, the G term on damage, and armour that scales with level. A Defence attribute would move numbers the gap already moves.
  • In the research, defence-as-a-stat lives in turn-based games (Fire Emblem, Pokémon). Action games keep it on gear or level, and the Kitchen Sink has no armour gear.
  • A robot's toughness reads best as its body (plating, the Riveter's front plate). That's data, like Monster Hunter, and it needs no stat.
  • Four means each shard's roll is obvious: "this time I'm a Dancer". Option B stays ready if the roll feels samey after your first phone pass.
  • Known tilt: DEX is the busiest stat, as Skill is in Fire Emblem. For a sword user a 1.2 DEX is worth about as much as a 1.2 STR (16 swings each). For a bow user DEX is the best roll. The roll is free and balanced, so that's fine.
