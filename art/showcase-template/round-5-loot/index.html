LOOT AND ECONOMY

Kitchen Sink (Showcase Template) · design proposal · 2026-11-02 · plan docs/plans/kitchen-sink-v2/loot-economy-mini-plan.md

You said loot, items and gear are under-thought, and they are. This page shows what loot actually does in each shard today, what eight good games do with it, and a design for the Kitchen Sink where every kill pays something you can see a use for. Six questions for you sit at the end.

THE SHORT VERSION

  1. The east earns, the west spends. Kill robots for Scrip (coins) and parts. Spend Scrip at Tinker Rook's stall on one-off upgrades, a third of them toys for the west half (hover, grapple).
  2. One part per robot type (Servo, Tine, Rotor, Nozzle, Rivet plate, Arc coil). Every kill drops one, no dice. Parts finish an upgrade's price and turn into ammo and patch kits at the bench.
  3. Weapon tiers become Forge I–IV: four one-off buys that raise every weapon at once. No item levels.
  4. Fixed rewards everywhere: every elite, mini-boss, chest and contract has a written prize. Six named night elites drop weapon finishes and trophies; the Crate Titan drops the Crate Cleaver.
  5. Grey kills pay nothing, Scrip and XP alike, so the next band is always the best place to be.

YOUR PICKS SO FAR (ALL KEPT)

  • The west half pays nothing: no XP, no coins.
  • Coins from kills, chests and kill contracts; the shop sells flat one-off upgrades (no item levels).
  • Loot is fixed rewards only (no random gear), no rarity tiers; the Crate Titan gives a legendary weapon; a 20-slot pack.
  • Collectibles that pay perks; trophies; skins and finishes as elite drops; joke-title feats.
  • Death costs XP toward the next level and never delevels.

Two readings are new: the levels page's weapon tiers become one-off Forge upgrades (Q2), and coins follow the XP gap rule instead of Driftwood's pay-once-per-enemy cap (Q3).

4 · THE PROPOSAL

Scrip, the one currency

The Foreman's company scrip, on the gold coin icon the mockups already use. A kill pays its type's rate, times the same gap factor as XP (grey, six levels under, pays 0), and night shift pays ×1.25. No per-enemy cap: bands respawn in 90 seconds, and the gap rule is what stops farming.

Parts, the salvage

Every robot drops exactly one part of its type (night shift two), and you collect it by walking over it, like coins. No kneeling: at 400+ kills a climb, a carve beat on every kill would wear thin on a phone (Pine keeps its kneel). Mini-bosses drop a core once each. A part's pack tile says what it is for.

Tinker Rook's stall, two tabs

UPGRADES: one-off buys for Scrip plus a few parts, unlocked by level, Driftwood's model. BENCH: parts into consumables, Pine's barter model. Welders give the nozzles for pitch bolts, and pitch burns the Riveters in the next band, so where you farm sets what you shoot.

The pack: 20 slots that never fill

One slot per kind: 6 parts, 3 cores, patch kits (carry 5), the vault keycard. Kinds you haven't found yet show as grey silhouettes, so the grid is also a "what's out there" board. Ammo stays in the pouch. No inventory juggling on a phone.

Trophies, finishes, collectibles, feats

  • Six named night elites, one per band, two levels up, out only after dark: Sir Nibbles (Crate Mite), Old Forky (Pallet Jack), Nailbiter (Plumb Drone), Hot Mess (Welder), Riveting Rita (Riveter), Static Steve (Arc Sentry). Each drops a weapon finish and a trophy.
  • Trophy wall in the hub, 12 frames: 6 elites, 3 mini-bosses, the Titan, 2 secret caches. A hung trophy's small perk is always on.
  • Gold 1×1s: 20 gold stencilled cubes, 10 west and 10 east. Every 5 make a Calibration Chip (+5 % max HP · dodge −15 % · sprint +10 % · weapons glow at night). Perks only, never XP or Scrip.
  • Feats: Scrap Metal Enthusiast (250 parts), Retail Therapist (every upgrade), Independent Contractor (16 contracts), Interior Decorator (12 trophies), Graveyard Shift (6 elites), Cube Collector, Patch Notes.

Why kill the next pack

  1. XP, the base.
  2. Scrip toward the next upgrade, which the stall shows locked with its level and price.
  3. This band's part counter on the pack tile ("Rotors 5 / 8 for Forge II").
  4. The band's contract on the board.
  5. Its named elite at night, and the finish it drops.

Death

Costs XP only (your rule). Scrip, parts and the pack are never lost.

8 · QUESTIONS FOR YOU

1. Parts? One salvage part per robot type, picked up by walking over it, used for upgrades and the bench. Recommended: yes.
2. Weapon tiers as Forge I–IV? Four one-off buys that raise every weapon, instead of buying each weapon's tiers. Recommended: yes (no item levels, and switching weapon costs nothing).
3. Grey kills pay 0 Scrip, with no per-enemy cap? The same rule as XP, instead of Driftwood's pay-once. Recommended: yes.
4. The Titan's legendary? The fixed Crate Cleaver sword, or a Titan Core you forge into the weapon you use most. Recommended: the Cleaver now; the core can come later.
5. Six named night elites? One per band, each dropping a finish and a trophy. Recommended: yes.
6. The currency's name? Scrip, coins or bolts. Recommended: Scrip (bolts are already ammo).
