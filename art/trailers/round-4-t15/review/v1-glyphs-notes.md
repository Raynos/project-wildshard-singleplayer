# V1 · Prompt to glyphs — what happens between the frames

- **0–0.5 s** fade up on a real Claude Code terminal (the asciinema recording, re-drawn glyph for glyph).
- **0.5–3.3 s** the prompt *a small island with a crooked lighthouse and a toon sea* types key by key at the recorded
  rhythm (2.5× speed); a block cursor blinks. Caption *DESCRIBE A WORLD.* under the terminal.
- **3.3–6.1 s** Enter; the real answer streams (squeezed ~5.6×): it reads the folder, writes `world.ts`, summarises the
  island (lean 8°, toon sea, two palms, rocks, rowboat). Ends on *Churned for 12s*.
- **6.55–8.8 s** every character on screen lifts off and arcs into a coarse monospace mosaic covering the frame,
  coloured by the painting underneath; landed glyphs lay a soft colour tile, so the island's shape appears in letters.
- **9.0–11.2 s** the cells resolve into the painting, spreading out from the lighthouse; slow 3.5 % push-in.
  Caption *CLAUDE CODE BUILDS IT.* from 9.7 s.
- **12–15 s** end card over the held island: the Cell logo, *Play it in the browser. Build it in Claude Code.*,
  wildshard.io, *Concept trailer · not gameplay*. Corner tag on 0–12 s.
- **Sound:** a minimal warm electronic cue (MiniMax take 501) blooming at ~10 s; real-sounding keyboard typing under the
  prompt, an Enter click, a glassy scatter as the glyphs fly (Stable Audio 3).
