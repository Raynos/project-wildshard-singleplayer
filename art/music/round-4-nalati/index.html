Instrument test

Can MiniMax play a dombra, a kobyz, a sybyzgy and a throat-sung drone convincingly?

Verdict (by measurement; your ear decides): Partly. The sybyzgy flute and the throat-sung drone come through clearly; the dombra partly; the kobyz does not. So the score describes each instrument by its sound, keeps the throat drone for the night and the King, and keeps the arrangements lean. Measured with CLAP, htdemucs and harmonic / percussive analysis, because nobody could listen: please listen before trusting it.

  • Sybyzgy (breathy wooden flute): yes: CLAP hears a wooden flute 99.6% of the time, the tone is harmonic and voiced 96% of the time, and the hummed drone under it is there (10% in the vocal stem).
  • Throat drone: yes: solo, CLAP hears throat singing 60% of the time (some flute on top). Under the night ensemble it is 96%, and the drone holds 60% of the energy. It goes into the night and King cues only.
  • Dombra (plucked two-string lute): partly: the take has a plucked, strummed attack (percussive energy 57% of the harmonic, the drum stem 24%), but CLAP hears a flute over it (84%). Listen for whether it is a dombra or a generic lute.
  • Kobyz (bowed horsehair fiddle): no: the tone is sustained, but CLAP hears a plucked lute (63%) and a jaw harp, not a raspy bowed fiddle (1%). MiniMax seems to read 'kobyz' as the plucked kobza. The score prompts ask for 'a raspy bowed horsehair fiddle' instead.
  • Full ensemble: drifts: the day ensemble picked up an orchestra (35% in a first CLAP pass) and 21% vocal energy, although the prompt said no vocals. The score's arrangements are leaner and say 'no vocals' again.

How the takes were ranked

Kazakh instruments 35%: the share CLAP gives the sounds of four Kazakh instruments (a plucked two-string lute, a raspy bowed fiddle, a breathy wooden flute, throat singing) against six foils (an orchestra, piano, acoustic guitar, synths, a rock band, a singer with words). CLAP does not know the instruments by name, so each is described by its sound. Groove 20%: drums and bass across the take (htdemucs), because the game's tension layer is exactly those stems. Loop seam 20%, clean audio 15%, full length with no fade-out 10%. A take whose separated vocal stem holds over 20% of the energy is out, except in the night and the King's slots, where the throat drone is wanted; there the limit is 60%.
