# P15 council, round 1 — Seat A (E472)

**Make: #1 The Crossing, #3 The Party, #6 The Dragon of Nine Dragon Stack, in that order.** They offer three different attractions: crossing into another style, adventuring together, and encountering something enormous.

## Evidence and bar

Read `ideas.md` and `council/ledger.md`; opened and inspected all six `shard-*.jpg` references and `board-draft1.jpg`.

The references supply distinct visual identities: Driftwood's faceted palms and turquoise water; Nalati's painterly mountains and riders; Pine's detailed forest and skeletal Antler King; Dunes' orange dusk and signal tower; Nine Dragon's wet neon streets; Sky Reach's golden floating islands.

The rejected board repeatedly returns to aerial plots and resets to another location. Its panels lack a consistently readable adventurer carrying the story. Preserve the beautiful environments, but make each cut follow a character's action or its consequence.

I use the brief's WoW bar: heroes, action, scale, one reveal. Makeable scores are production judgments based on the ledger's stated LTX limits, not verified animation results.

## Pick 1 — The Crossing

**Why:** the strongest combination of excitement and a promise specific to this game.

| Shot | Seconds | Action and movement |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 4 | Wide side view: the Nalati rider canters toward a highway junction; scarf, dust and horse move together. Establish rider and mount clearly. |
| 2 | 3 | Medium-wide flank tracking on the highway approach to Driftwood. The horse passes behind a foreground roadside post; cut during the brief occlusion. |
| 3 | 4 | Continue the same direction and framing in Driftwood's faceted style. Horse and rider emerge beyond the post and canter onto the beach approach; sand kicks up. |
| 4 | 4 | Camera rises as the rider continues below, revealing floating square lands connected by two-lane highways and roundabouts. Title overlays the final 2 seconds. |

**The selling image:** the faceted rider and horse emerging onto turquoise Driftwood, with painterly Nalati visible behind the connecting highway.

**Riskiest shot and staging:** the shot 2–3 match. Use the same character sheet, horse markings, tack, scarf and screen direction in both styles. Keep faces small and bodies in profile; cut behind the post rather than matching exposed hooves. No generated morph, rear or simultaneous camera rotation.

**One improvement:** replace the proposed centre-line trigger with the actual road-to-shard boundary. A highway's dashed centre line should remain a traffic marking; the destination itself motivates the change.

## Pick 2 — The Party

**Why:** the most direct picture of players from different worlds sharing an adventure.

| Shot | Seconds | Action and movement |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 3 | Rear three-quarter wide: all four adventurers walk together along the highway toward Pine Hollow. Their silhouettes and clothing remain distinguishable. |
| 2 | 4 | Front three-quarter view: they take a few measured steps, then stop as a huge antler shadow crosses them. The pirate raises an already-held blade. |
| 3 | 5 | Reverse wide, party visible along the foreground: the Antler King slowly raises its skull above the forest edge; its glowing ribs brighten. |
| 4 | 3 | Medium-wide party response: the pirate takes one step forward; companions turn toward the threat and brace with weapons already in hand. Title overlays the final 2 seconds. |

**The selling image:** four visibly different rendering styles standing together beneath antlers wider than the highway.

**Riskiest shot and staging:** the mixed-style lineup in shot 2. Establish all four styles in the first keyframe, with clear spacing and separate silhouettes. Use slow movement and a nearly fixed camera; avoid overlapping bodies, close faces and weapons crossing another character. Darkness must not erase the style differences.

**One improvement:** give the party a shared decision: the pirate advances and the others back them. That small action makes them companions confronting danger, rather than four character samples posing together.

## Pick 3 — The Dragon of Nine Dragon Stack

**Why:** the strongest production prospect for a large cinematic reveal. It complements the other two by selling danger and scale.

| Shot | Seconds | Action and movement |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 4 | Wide rear view: one rider trots from a highway junction toward Nine Dragon's rain-soaked gate. Keep an adjacent contrasting shard visible beyond the road. |
| 2 | 3 | Rider and horse remain visible beneath the gate as lanterns swing and dust falls. The rider looks upward; a rumble interrupts the hoofbeat rhythm. |
| 3 | 5 | Wide city view: a colossal tiled dragon head and upper neck rise slowly between two fixed towers. The rider remains tiny at their base. |
| 4 | 3 | Ground-level wide: the dragon lowers its head toward the road as the rider canters away across the foreground. Title overlays the final 2 seconds. |

**The selling image:** a wet tiled dragon head towering between neon buildings, with one mounted traveller beneath it for scale.

**Riskiest shot and staging:** the creature emergence in shot 3. Start with the head and upper neck already partly visible in the keyframe. Animate a slow upward translation and small head tilt; conceal the remaining body behind buildings and rain. Keep towers rigid. Do not attempt a full serpentine uncoil or an orbit around the creature.

**One improvement:** make the dragon's material belong to Nine Dragon: dark roof tiles, restrained lantern light and wet surfaces. A generic glowing dragon would weaken the city's identity.

## Traps and production constraints

- **#2 is the chief production trap.** Six styles require roughly six independently controlled action takes; four shots cannot deliver the stated hook reliably. Shorter cuts would hide faults while making the trailer harder to read.
- **#7 is a motion trap.** A six-second duel depends on exactly the fast limbs, contacts and spatial continuity the ledger identifies as weak.
- **#8 is an audience trap.** The map transformation spends precious seconds returning to the builder trailer's visual language.
- **#4 is the reserve, not a first-session pick.** It offers a different thrill, but freefall, changing environments and opening fabric stack several difficult motions into one beat.

All three picks total **15 seconds each**, with four generated shots apiece. Carry the same hero or party through every shot; use cuts motivated by action, restrained hero detail, a rising musical cue and one decisive reveal. Keep **“Concept trailer · not gameplay”** on every frame, including the end card. Composite titles in the edit.

**Verdict:** make The Crossing first, The Party second, and The Dragon third. Protect character continuity and visible action before adding more worlds or spectacle.
