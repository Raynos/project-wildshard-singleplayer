Jake rejected there: fifteen beautiful frames, each its own world, no hero carried through, nothing happening. Each
frame works as a postcard and none of them as a scene. The bar (WoW 2004) is one cast, bodies in motion, one big
reveal. So I score down any idea whose structure is "one clip per world": however it is dressed up, it comes out as

## Traps

- **#2 One swing, six worlds.** It sounds like a match cut, but it is six 2–3 s LTX takes (over the 4-shot budget),
  and the model cannot place a sword arc. Unless the arcs line up exactly it is a montage of unrelated clips, which is
  exactly the rejected CT2. The likeliest of the ten to look "AI clips put together".
- **#7 Border Duel.** The whole trailer is close combat, the one motion LTX smears. No staging saves it.
- **#8, #9, #10.** All three are a camera moving over worlds, or a montage of one clip per world. #8 also reuses the
  builder trailer's ink look. They are well made and forgettable.
- **#6 is not a trap but a stand-in.** It is the most makeable money shot, but it shows one world and so does not
  carry the promise. Keep it as the fallback reveal if a pick fails its test roll.

## Pick 1 — The Crossing (the promise in one cut)

| Shot | s | What moves |
|------|---|------------|
| A | 4 | Nalati golden hour (the look in `shard-nalati.jpg`). The rider gallops at three-quarters toward the camera, mid-wide; the camera tracks back; grass whips; an eagle overhead. |
| B | 3 | Low side-on wide. Where painted grass meets the highway, the horse **leaps the white line** in slow motion; **cut at the top of the leap**. |
| C | 4 | The same top of the leap and the same direction of travel, now **faceted toon** (the look in `shard-driftwood.jpg`). They come down in Driftwood surf, spray bursting, and gallop on along the beach road. |
| D | 4 | Crane up and back from the small rider. The highway runs on to the horizon through shard after shard in different styles; sails, a roc and the rider keep moving. Title over the last 2 s. |

- **The image that sells it:** the C keyframe. The faceted toon horse lands in a wall of turquoise spray, the red
  cloak streaming, in exactly the pose of B one cut earlier.
- **Riskiest shot: C (the same rider read across styles).** Make one character sheet with **both** styles side by side
  in a single OpenAI image and use it as the reference for A, B and C. Give the hero identity anchors that survive any
  style: a **white horse and a long red cloak**. Generate B's last frame first, then C's keyframe with B's frame as a
  second reference so the pose matches. Keep the camera locked and side-on through B→C so the cut reads as continuous
  motion. For C, ask for the landing and galloping on, not a rear: rearing legs smear, and spray hides hooves.
- **One change:** replace the ideas' close shot beside the hooves (fast limbs in close-up, LTX's worst case) with
  **the leap over the line, cut at the top of the leap**. A body at the top of a jump is a rigid, tucked shape; it
  holds, and a mid-air cut is the classic match cut.

## Pick 2 — The Party (heroes and a monster: the WoW structure)

| Shot | s | What moves |
|------|---|------------|
| A | 2 | Low tracking shot along the dashed line at dusk: four pairs of feet walking (toon boots, painted felt boots, photoreal leather, neon-wet sandals). |
| B | 5 | Slow dolly back ahead of the line-up walking toward camera, wide, backlit by the sunset: toon pirate, painterly horsewoman, photoreal pine ranger, neon samurai. Cloaks and hair move. |
| C | 2 | They stop. The dashed line shivers and a vast shadow sweeps across them; four heads tilt up. |
| D | 5 | Reverse, low from behind them: the **Antler King** (the look in `shard-pine.jpg`, glowing ribs) rises out of the pine shard beyond the roundabout, colossal, antlers through the canopy. They draw weapons as silhouettes small in frame. Title on the last beat. |

- **The image that sells it:** D. Four small backlit silhouettes, one per style, square up on the asphalt to a
  photoreal colossus filling the sky.
- **Riskiest shot: B (four styles kept apart).** One prompt tends to blend everything into one style. Make each hero
  from their own sheet, composite the four into one keyframe plate (so it is still one first frame), and give each a
  different light: the pirate under warm flat cel light, the samurai under neon rim light. Keep LTX slow: walking
  pace, the camera barely moving, the figures at knee-to-head height in a wide frame, so faces stay small.
- **One change:** end on action, not a cut to black. As the title hits, the four **break into a run toward it**
  (wide, from behind, silhouettes). Also have the beast rise **across the shard border**, so the frame shows one
  world's monster towering over a party from four others. The variety of styles becomes the stakes.

## Pick 3 — Leap of Faith (scale: the world is a sky full of lands)

| Shot | s | What moves |
|------|---|------------|
| A | 3 | Sky Reach island edge at sunset (the look in `shard-skyreach.jpg`). Wide from behind: the runner sprints away from the camera across the grass and leaps off the edge, a silhouette against the cloud sea. |
| B | 5 | Top-down, the camera falling with her: a small, rigid spread-eagle figure falling away from us. Below, the **grid** comes up to meet her: floating square shards, each its own style (desert, pines, neon, beach), the highway between them. A roc glides beside her for scale. |
| C | 5 | Cut on the opening of the glider. She swoops in fast and low over Nine Dragon's rain-slick neon rooftops and lanterns (the look in `shard-ninedragon.jpg`), the camera chasing behind; lantern strings whip past. |
| — | 2 | End card. |

- **The image that sells it:** B. A tiny figure in freefall over a patchwork of floating worlds in six styles, the
  single scale reveal the WoW bar asks for, and the clearest picture of "an endless grid" in the whole set.
- **Riskiest shot: B (the freefall).** Lock the camera to her so she stays rigid and centred, and let the world move
  only by a **slow push in** (the grid getting bigger), which LTX handles. No sideways whipping past islands, which
  melts. Shoot her from above, back to the camera, with no face. Make the roc a gliding shape (wings set, no
  flapping).
- **One change:** never show the glider opening. It changes shape mid-shot, which LTX breaks. Start C with the glider
  already open, already swooping. And make B the grid seen from above, not islands at random: the fall *is* the
  reveal that this is one world of many.

## Why these three together

Each says the promise differently: **across** (a horizontal ride through a border), **together** (four worlds' heroes
against one), **down** (vertical scale over the whole grid). None reuses another's reveal. That is 4 + 4 + 3 = 11
LTX shots, inside the ledger's ~12, with re-rolls going to C of the Crossing and B of the Party.

If a test roll of a riskiest shot fails, swap in **#6 the Dragon**, made cross-world: the dragon uncoils out of Nine
Dragon and its body arcs over the highway into the next shard.
