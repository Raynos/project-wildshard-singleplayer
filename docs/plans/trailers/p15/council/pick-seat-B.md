# P15 council, round 1: seat B

Read: `ideas.md`, `ledger.md`, the six `round-2-highway/shard-*.jpg` and `round-3-draft1/board-draft1.jpg`.

**What the images taught me.** The six looks are not equally far apart. Driftwood (faceted low-poly toon), Pine
Hollow (photoreal) and Nine Dragon (neon rain) are clearly different from each other. Nalati and Sky Reach are both
golden-hour painterly, and next to each other they read as one style. So any style-change beat should cross a **far**
pair (painterly → toon, toon → photoreal, golden → neon), never Nalati → Sky Reach. The rejected draft also shows the
trap to avoid: half its frames are top-down grid pull-outs (7, 14, 6) and lone postcards with a caption. Any idea that
ends on "pull up over the grid, worlds beyond" is ending on the shot Jake already called AI clips.

## Traps

- **#2 One swing, six worlds**: on paper it's a match cut, but on screen it's six unrelated AI clips at 0.5–1 s each,
  which is the rejected montage at double speed. LTX can't match a sword arc across six separately generated takes,
  and a smeared blade at speed is the clearest AI tell there is.
- **#7 Border Duel**: close fighting, two faces and fast limbs. Every frame sits where LTX is weakest.
- **#8 The Map**: it's the builder trailer's look, and the reveal is a dissolve. It echoes V3/V4 ("just images",
  "not a moving trailer").
- **The grid pull-out ending** (in #1, #8, #9): don't end any player trailer on it (see above).

## Picks

### Pick 1: The Crossing (painterly → toon, one rider)
This is the only idea whose central image is new, and it *is* the promise: you ride into a land and become part of
its art. The Party and Leap both say "many worlds"; this one says "the world changes you".

| Shot | s | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | Nalati at dusk, wide on a long lens: a lone rider gallops toward the camera down the steppe track to the highway, kicking up dust. The rider stays small in frame. |
| 2 | 3 | Side-on mid-shot, camera tracking with the rider in slow motion (half speed): horse and rider pass behind a roadside highway post in the foreground. **Hard cut** on the frame where the post covers them. |
| 3 | 4 | Same framing and speed, now in Driftwood's faceted toon style: they come out from behind the matching post onto the beach, the horse rears in silhouette against the sky, and toon surf explodes. |
| 4 | 4 | The toon rider wheels and gallops at the *next* border, where photoreal Pine Hollow pines and fog stand. Cut to black the instant the hoof touches that line. Then the title. |

- **Image that sells it:** shot 3's keyframe. A faceted toon horse rears in the surf, and behind it, past the white
  line, the painterly steppe still glows. Two styles in one frame, split by the road.
- **Riskiest shot and staging:** the lead's hoof close-up (shot 2). Swap it for a hidden cut behind a foreground
  post: side-on, slow motion, a rigid post covering the seam. Both takes are generated from keyframes that match in
  pose and framing, so the cut lands on the post and not on smeared legs. If a hoof insert is wanted, make it a single
  hoof planting in slow motion, 1.5 s, with dust in painterly strokes, cut on impact.
- **One change:** drop the closing grid pull-out. End on the **second** crossing about to happen (toon → photoreal)
  and cut to black. It says "this happens again at every border", it's endless without a map shot, and it avoids the
  rejected draft's ending.

### Pick 2: The Party (four styles, one monster)
This is the closest to WoW 2004: heroes, a hero walk, scale and a monster reveal. Four styles in one frame is the
image people will screenshot.

| Shot | s | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | Low, behind them: four pairs of boots and hooves on the highway's dashed line at dusk, walking slowly. |
| 2 | 5 | Low wide from **behind and to the side**: the four walk away from the camera toward the next shard. Toon pirate, painterly horsewoman, photoreal ranger, neon samurai. Silhouettes and costumes do the work; no faces. The camera pushes slowly. |
| 3 | 3 | They stop. A huge shadow sweeps over them and the road. Only the light changes: four rigid figures and one moving shadow. |
| 4 | 5 | Reverse, wide from low behind them: the photoreal Antler King rises out of Pine Hollow's mist, antlers against the sky and ribs glowing. The four draw weapons in strong poses. Cut to black on the drawn blades, then the title. |

- **Image that sells it:** shot 4, four small figures in four styles, backs to us, facing a beast ten times their
  height.
- **Riskiest shot and staging:** shot 2. Generate a character sheet for each of the four in its own style. Compose
  the line-up keyframe using all four sheets as references, each lit with its own world's colour (warm toon sun, amber
  paint, cool real light, magenta neon) so the styles stay separate. Reject any keyframe where they blend. A slow walk
  away from the camera keeps legs and faces out of LTX's weak zone.
- **One change:** shoot the walk from behind, not as the lead's push across their faces. Heroes seen from behind,
  facing the reveal, are the WoW grammar, and it removes the riskiest content (four close faces) at no cost.

### Pick 3: Leap of Faith (vertical, a sky full of worlds)
Vertigo and scale, and the motion is the opposite of the other two (a fall, not a ride or a walk). A body falling in
a rigid skydiver pose is LTX-friendly.

| Shot | s | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Sky Reach, side-on wide: she sprints along a mossy island's edge and leaps out into the clouds. Hold on the silhouette at the top of the leap. |
| 2 | 4 | Top-down, camera falling with her: she's spread-eagled and rigid in the centre of frame. Far below is a stack of lands in different styles at once (a toon island, a painterly steppe square, and Nine Dragon's neon glow at the bottom). It's one continuous push in toward a fixed composition, with nothing new entering the frame. |
| 3 | 4 | Low over Nine Dragon's rooftops in rain: her glider snaps open and she swoops between the towers, lanterns swinging in her wake. |
| 4 | 4 | Wide: as she banks past the gate, **a neon-and-tile dragon uncoils between the towers** beside her, a small silhouette. Hold on the scale, then the title. |

- **Image that sells it:** shot 2, looking straight down past her boots at a sky stacked with square worlds in
  different styles.
- **Riskiest shot and staging:** shot 2. LTX won't spawn worlds "whipping past" mid-take, so put every world in the
  first frame and make the move a push-in (the lands grow, and parallax does the vertigo). She stays rigid at the
  centre with only cloth and hair moving. Shot 4's dragon is a slow, rigid-segmented rise in a wide shot, not a fast
  coil.
- **One change:** fold idea #6's dragon in as Leap's reveal. As written, Leap has no reveal (a glider opening isn't
  one) and #6 has only one world. Together they get the cross-world journey *and* the monster money shot.

## Why these three together
They differ in **motion** (gallop, walk, fall), in **promise** (the world changes you; heroes from every world; a sky
of worlds), and in **reveal** (the transformation, the Antler King, the dragon). Each carries one hero or party
through every shot, cuts on motion, and spends LTX on wide shots, slow motion, silhouettes and rigid giants. That's
twelve shots, the ledger's budget, with re-rolls going to Crossing shot 3 and Party shot 2.
