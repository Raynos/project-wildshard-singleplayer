# T15 council, round 1 — Seat A (E471)

**Pick #2 Describe a world, #3 The map that draws itself, #5 Postcards, and #16 The signpost.** These offer four different arguments and techniques: making a place, growing a shared world, inviting an author, and travelling between authors’ worlds.

The rejected board’s repeated aerial grids, sunset cliffs and illuminated fantasy cities already feel interchangeable as stills. Removing generated motion alone will not fix that. The six separate shard paintings preserve stronger differences, especially Driftwood’s facets against Nalati’s brushwork and Pine Hollow’s forest detail. Keep those differences; avoid grading everything into the same cinematic sunset.

## Four to make

### 1. #2 — Describe a world: real capture and one held illustration

- **Why it wins:** It demonstrates Wildshard’s unusual editor directly. A stranger can connect a person’s sentence to a specific place without knowing what a shard is.
- **15 seconds:** 0–4: the real Claude Code prompt, “A toon island with a lighthouse.” 4–6: a brief, genuine build response. 6–12: hard cut to the matching concept illustration; two neighbouring styles and their connecting highway remain visible. Caption: “Your shard. Built in Claude Code.” 12–15: end card.
- **Riskiest step:** Capturing an authentic interaction that reads at phone size. Show a tight terminal crop with one prompt and one meaningful response; streaming logs are texture, not required reading.
- **Concrete improvement:** Replace “shard uploaded” with actual recorded output. The visual transition should express the concept, without inventing a successful upload or introducing an upload ritual.
- **Technique boundary:** Real capture plus a still. A restrained 2D push is sufficient; depth reconstruction adds little here.

### 2. #3 — The map that draws itself: graphic animation

- **Why it wins:** It explains that separate contributions become one connected world. Visible strokes, deliberate spacing and different drawing styles provide a strong alternative to cinematic paintings.
- **15 seconds:** 0–4: one square island is drawn. 4–9: three neighbours appear, each with different marks, joined by a recognisable two-lane highway and roundabouts. 9–12: the map extends beyond the frame, leaving an outlined plot labelled “Yours.” Caption: “Each shard built by a player in Claude Code.” 12–15: end card.
- **Riskiest step:** Preserving distinct places and a readable road as the map grows. Use SVG paths and a few composed illustrations rather than procedural visual clutter.
- **Concrete improvement:** Sharpen the original from “thousands of plots” to four readable contributions and an unfinished edge. The edge suggests expansion without a numerical claim.
- **Technique boundary:** Entirely authored motion design; no generated motion or Blender scene.

### 3. #5 — Postcards: tactile flat layers

- **Why it wins:** The blank card makes the viewer an author. Its physical metaphor and small gag distinguish it from both the terminal demonstration and the abstract map.
- **15 seconds:** 0–3: Driftwood’s card lands. 3–8: Nalati and Pine Hollow cards settle beside it, with enough time to see their different styles. 8–12: a blank fourth card slides into the arrangement; “Yours” is written across it. Caption: “Build your shard in Claude Code.” 12–15: end card.
- **Riskiest step:** Making card motion feel intentional rather than like presentation slides. Animate a few measured rotations and settling movements; use consistent contact shadows.
- **Concrete improvement:** Reduce six dealt cards to three, arranged on a printed highway map. The fourth card completes a visible road connection, so this sells a shared world rather than a gallery.
- **Technique boundary:** Flat illustrations and designed handwriting; no generated hand. A simple paper surface avoids spending the session on a realistic wooden tabletop.

### 4. #16 — The signpost: one painting and one animated insert

- **Why it wins:** It gives the highway a purpose: other players’ worlds are destinations, and yours can become one. The new arrow is one memorable event rather than a sequence of spectacle shots.
- **15 seconds:** 0–4: a close view of two existing arrows with small destination illustrations. 4–7: a new arrow slides into place: “Your shard.” 7–12: a modest widening reveals two distinct neighbouring worlds and their roundabout. Caption: “Every shard built by a player in Claude Code.” 12–15: end card.
- **Riskiest step:** The widening move exposing missing depth behind the sign. Compose the complete reveal first, separate the sign as a foreground layer, and keep camera displacement small.
- **Concrete improvement:** Sharpen the original to two existing destinations plus yours. Use clear type added in post and a recognisable landmark on each arrow; unfamiliar shard names alone cannot carry the gag.
- **Technique boundary:** Blender used only to film a painting and its cut-out layers is within the ledger’s judgement-call allowance. Do not build a final-look 3D crossroads scene.

## Traps and execution bar

- **#7 is the largest trap:** it promises to cure an AI reel with another generated take, while requiring the least proven motion and style control.
- **#9 and #20 are finish traps:** their appealing descriptions hide rigging and perspective-matching work that can consume the whole session before typography and editing begin.
- **#13 and #14 invite the exact rejection already received:** stock portals and generated hands make the technique more noticeable than Wildshard.
- **#4 is a permitted single-object judgement call, but I would decline it:** extraction, backside repair and polished studio rendering are too uncertain for this comparison.
- **#12 is the strongest clarity benchmark, but does not take a slot:** the selected map already tests graphic authorship, while postcards and the signpost offer more distinctive memories.
- All four must work muted, keep essential action and captions readable in an iPhone portrait presentation, and carry “Concept trailer · not gameplay” on every frame and the end card.
- Reserve 12–15 seconds for the Project Wildshard logo and `wildshard.io`. Sound supports timing; it carries no essential explanation.

**Verdict:** Make #2, #3, #5 and #16 with the stated scope reductions. Compare a demonstration, a growing map, a tactile invitation and a destination gag—not four cinematic grid flyovers.
