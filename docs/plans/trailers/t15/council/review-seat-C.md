**V1 · Prompt to glyphs**
- *Reads muted:* the two captions alone say *DESCRIBE A WORLD. / CLAUDE CODE BUILDS IT.*, the clearest Claude Code
  message of the four. But it shows one island, with no grid, no neighbours and no other players, so "a world of
  shards built by players" is left to the end card. The terminal fills only the top ~45 % of the frame at its natural
  size (0.5–6.5 s), which is not the tight crop that was asked for, so on a phone the prompt line is about 2 mm tall.
- *Not AI:* the real terminal is plainly real. The glyph flight (7.5 s) and the mosaic (8.5 s) are designed and grid-
  locked, and nothing morphs. The island (10.5 s) is a clean, flat Driftwood toon in midday light with few objects,
  the opposite of draft 1. Two small tells: the lighthouse is barely crooked, so the result doesn't quite match the
  prompt, and the 3.5 % push-in on a still is the only drift.
- *Finish:* there are two visible faults. At 4.5 s the full scratchpad path shows
  (`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-raynos-projects-games-wildshard-singleplayer/9acb64f7-…`), which looks like a leaked
  dev session. From 12.5 to 14.5 s, *CLAUDE CODE BUILDS IT.* is still faintly visible under the end card, which looks
  like a missed fade. Mosaic cells also linger at the left edge at 10.5 s.
- *Memorable:* the frame where the island appears out of letters (8.5–9.5 s). It is the one image here that belongs
  only to "built by typing".

**V2 · The map that draws itself**
- *Reads muted:* the grid that keeps filling, plus the *yours* plot, says "many plots, one free" well. But the only
  words before the end card are a small handwritten line in a bottom-left box, fully written only at 11.35 s, which
  leaves about 0.6 s to read it before the end card. Claude Code never appears before 12 s. The signatures (*@mara* …)
  can't be read even in my 2× crop.
- *Not AI:* ink on paper, drawn stroke by stroke, with designed timing. Nothing boils. The tell is in the vignettes
  (Ferris wheel and circus tent, the castle on a hill, 8.5–11.5 s): they look like clip-art or generated line art, and
  the "different hands" mostly read as one pen in two inks (blue and black). Crayon, charcoal and stipple don't show
  at this size.
- *Finish:* the weakest of the four. From 6.6 to 9.9 s the cartouche is a blank grey rectangle over the bottom-left
  plots (7.5, 8.5, 9.5 s), which reads as a hole in the render. The highway reads as a dotted-path grid, not a two-lane
  road, and the vermilion centre dash is invisible once the camera pulls back (from 5.5 s on). The handwritten caption
  ghosts under the end card (12.5–14.5 s). At 2.5 s a roundabout is clipped at the top-left edge.
- *Memorable:* the full sheet at 11.5 s with the red *yours*; pleasant, but like many "map fills in" explainers.

**V3 · Postcards**
- *Reads muted:* four very different worlds land on one road map, then a blank card types *your world*. That gets
  across "many worlds, yours is next". "Built by a player" sits only in the *BUILT BY @…* strips, which are unreadable
  on a phone. The only Claude Code cue is the `>` prompt glyph, which reads to developers and to no one else.
- *Not AI:* the board is clearly designed: a locked camera, real contact shadows, a legend, plot numbers. The tell is
  the cards themselves. A "vintage travel postcard" painting is a favourite default of image generators, and four
  generated pictures in four styles is draft 1's montage with a frame round it. The Nine Dragon card (5.5 s on) is
  murky blue-grey and has the over-detailed, everything-filled-in look the art brief bans.
- *Finish:* the cleanest of the four. The card cadence is on the beat and the type is crisp. Flaws: the legend box
  takes up a whole plot, so the grid reads as a 2 × 3 board game rather than an endless grid. The planned roads that
  "print in" at 7.5–8.2 s don't show at all. The yellow highways read as Monopoly, not as a road. On the end card the
  dimmed cards still compete with the logo, because *NALATI* is legible behind the wordmark at 13.5 s.
- *Memorable:* the blank card with the cursor typing *your world* (10.5–11.5 s).

**V4 · The seam**
- *Reads muted:* a coastal road with a caption. Nothing on screen says "shard" (no plots, no cubes, no grid). *Someone
  else* is vague, and could even be read as "not you". Claude Code appears only on the end card.
- *Not AI:* draft 1's frame 9 ("the drive", 38 s) is the same composition: a two-lane road down the middle, palms on
  the right, streetlights, the vanishing point centred. The person who rejected draft 1 will recognise it. In the
  6.5 s crop the left verge is photographic grass, not an oil painting, and the asphalt is photoreal, so the "seam"
  doesn't read as two styles. It reads as a stock road photo with a few low-poly rocks pasted on.
- *Finish:* technically clean. The caption is large and centred, it fades out by 11.6 s so the end card is clean, and
  there are no ghosts. But the 0.6 m push looks still on the sheet (0.5 s vs 11.5 s differ only by a slight crop),
  so on a phone it will play as 12 s of a still image. The low-poly palm at the top right is cut off by the frame.
- *Memorable:* little. It's a pleasant road, and draft 1 already had one.

## Ranking

1. **V1** has the real thing on screen and the clearest pitch, and its look is furthest from draft 1.
2. **V3** is the best finished and says "many worlds, yours next", but its cards are where the AI tells sit.
3. **V2** has the right idea and the right technique, with the weakest finish and a caption no one will read in time.
4. **V4** comes closest to the draft Jake rejected and says the least.

## For wildshard.io

*Build it in Claude Code*, and V1 alone shows that as fact (a real session, prompt and answer). It is the hardest to
call AI, and its weakness (one island) is exactly what the alpha trailer covers.
