## V1 · Prompt to glyphs

- **Reads muted on a phone — 2/5:** “Describe a world” → “Claude Code builds it” communicates creation, but the tiny terminal at 0.5–6.5 s and isolated island at 11.5 s never explain the connected, player-built world.
- **Not AI — 4/5:** The real terminal and deliberate glyph assembly at 7.5–9.5 s feel authored; the pristine lighthouse illustration at 11.5 s still resembles a generic generated island, but avoids draft 1’s overloaded fantasy montage.
- **Finish — 3/5:** The transformation has a clear destination, but diagnostic text occupies much of the opening, the build caption remains faintly visible under the 12.5–14.5 s end card, and the corner tag is extremely small.
- **Memorable — 4/5:** The coloured characters becoming a crooked lighthouse at 9.5 s are the strongest single transformation among the four.

**Fixes, most important first:**

1. **0.5–6.5 s:** Enlarge and crop the existing terminal around the prompt and active response; remove the directory header and idle margins from the crop. Preserve the real recording.
2. **9.7–12 s:** Change the large caption to “YOUR SHARD. BUILT WITH CLAUDE CODE.” The current wording assigns all agency to the tool and never names the player’s contribution.
3. **12–15 s:** Remove the outgoing build caption before darkening the island; bring the complete end card in together by 12.3 s and enlarge the URL and concept disclosure.

## V2 · The map that draws itself

- **Reads muted on a phone — 3/5:** The road-connected plots, different drawings and red “yours” explain the world best, but the author signatures shrink away and the player caption finishes only around 11.35 s.
- **Not AI — 4/5:** The ruled roads and controlled drawing sequence at 0.5–6.5 s feel designed; the increasingly dense, similarly framed miniatures at 8.5–11.5 s weaken the impression of distinct human hands.
- **Finish — 3/5:** Paper, ink and road geometry form a coherent treatment, but the pullback sacrifices readable details and leaves the key explanation too late; the end card and corner disclosure need larger type.
- **Memorable — 4/5:** At 11.5 s, the red empty plot marked “yours” within a map of other worlds is a clear invitation with a recognisable visual identity.

**Fixes, most important first:**

1. **6.6–11.35 s:** Start the cartouche text earlier and finish it by 8.5 s; use “Every shard built by a player with Claude Code.” Enlarge the cartouche and lettering so it survives the pullback.
2. **8–12 s:** Reduce the final pullback and omit the outermost plot reveals. Keep the original neighbours and “yours” large enough to recognise; let the roads continue beyond the frame to suggest expansion.
3. **12–15 s:** Show the full end card together by 12.3 s, enlarge its URL and disclosure, and increase the content-frame corner tag’s size.

## V3 · Postcards

- **Reads muted on a phone — 3/5:** Four distinct destinations plus “your world” establish variety and participation, but the tiny “BUILT BY” strips cannot carry player authorship and Claude Code appears only on the end card.
- **Not AI — 4/5:** The consistent printed cards, borders and map at 1.5–11.5 s look like an editorial composition; the Nalati and Nine Dragon pictures retain some stock generated-landscape flavour, contained by the print treatment.
- **Finish — 3/5:** The layout stays stable and the blank card supplies a payoff, but small labels compete with the pictures and the lower row reaches the frame edge; sampled frames cannot establish whether the throws and shadows land convincingly.
- **Memorable — 4/5:** The blank postcard typing “your world” at 10.5–11.5 s is inviting and easy to recall.

**Fixes, most important first:**

1. **0–8 s:** Add one large caption in an unused map area: “PLAYER-BUILT SHARDS. ONE CONNECTED WORLD.” The postcard author strips are supporting detail, not a readable explanation.
2. **8.15–12 s:** Finish “your world” by 9.5 s and add a short second line, “Build yours with Claude Code,” on the blank card; leave both held before the end card.
3. **12–15 s:** Bring the full end card in together by 12.3 s and enlarge the URL and concept disclosure; enlarge the corner tag throughout the content.

## V4 · The seam

- **Reads muted on a phone — 3/5:** The contrasting verges and readable caption explain neighbouring styles, but “someone else” is vague, Claude Code arrives late, and the road never establishes the grid or floating shard structure.
- **Not AI — 2/5:** At 0.5–4.5 s, the smooth central road divides a highly realistic steppe from a polygonal beach like a generated comparison image; the calmer lighting improves on draft 1, but the painting still carries the result.
- **Finish — 3/5:** The composition and caption are clean, but over five seconds pass before the explanation appears, the sampled progression feels slight, and the small shared end card loses the final opportunity to clarify the pitch.
- **Memorable — 4/5:** The highway between steppe and toon sea at 5.5 s is an immediately recognisable image of contrasting worlds.

**Fixes, most important first:**

1. **1–11.6 s:** Start the caption at 1 s and change it to “ONE HIGHWAY. PLAYER-BUILT SHARDS.” Follow at 6 s with “BUILD YOURS WITH CLAUDE CODE.” Use the existing caption treatment.
2. **0–12 s:** Reframe the existing projection slightly upward to give the two landscapes more space and reduce the foreground asphalt. Keep the restrained camera move rather than increasing depth displacement.
3. **12–15 s:** Show the complete end card together by 12.3 s and enlarge the URL and disclosure; increase the corner tag’s size.

This falls on the permitted **camera-on-a-painting** side of the Blender distinction: the notes describe projection of one painting, not a modelled whole-scene final look. My objection is to the visible result, not that technique. The sheets cannot rule out projection stretching between samples.
