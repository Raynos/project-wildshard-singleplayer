## Individual panels: actual framing, direction, observations

Every source panel includes the full projected character without edge clipping. The
fixed orthographic scale leaves more side whitespace in profile; therefore profile
narrowness is genuine geometry/occlusion, not a crop. Side-head hiding is not proof of
missing parts. Camera elevation changes how much throat can be seen.

1. **Hero, azimuth +28°, elevation 15°:** front three-quarter view. All three pitchers
   separate clearly. Lids float high on narrow visible supports, with broad air gaps;
   their near-horizontal, thin green surfaces do not reproduce the hero's broad raised
   hood, substantial red underside or flared living hinge. The central red body is
   recognizable but reads as a regular bucket. Trunk and roots are large blunt wedges.
2. **Front, 0°/0°:** straight frontal orthographic. Central dominance and staggered side
   heights work. Lids are nearly edge-on green lines; mouth openings are largely hidden
   at this level. Equal-spaced external teeth dominate instead of thick irregular lip
   lobes. Root base reads as a pointed fan rather than an intertwined root arch.
3. **Left, −90°/0°:** camera on native −X. The forward/opening axis points **screen-right**;
   the rear woody keel is screen-left. Side vessels overlap into a narrow column. Thin
   lid supports and single-plane canopy construction are especially exposed.
4. **Right, +90°/0°:** camera on native +X. Forward axis points **screen-left**; rear keel
   is screen-right. Again a narrow layered column with a flat cap, uniform red vessel
   facets and little organic transition between vessel, supporting wood and lid.
5. **Back, 180°/0°:** rear timber keels establish the rear direction. Three red bodies and
   their circular mouths still exist, with exposed stick-like canopy supports. The
   largely bare rear trunk and evenly fanned roots lack the hero's dense grown structure;
   exact hidden-back decoration remains interpretive rather than mandatory.
6. **Top, 0°/89.9°:** almost vertical, **not mathematically exact 90°**. Three green
   polygonal caps dominate and obscure nearly all mouths. Roots make a regular starburst.
   Supporting AI top panels show more open lip/throat, but that difference is not a
   strict camera-matched metric. The hero already establishes the missing hood form.
7. **Elevated front, 0°/39°:** all three dark interior chambers are visible and genuinely
   dimensional. This is a strength. However, their thin uniform rim rings and repeated
   external triangular teeth contrast with the hero's rolled, bulky irregular lip,
   long descending ivory front lobes and jagged magenta inner throat. Sparse small leaves
   and tiny scattered barnacles leave the central wood unusually bare.
8. **Elevated left, −90°/39°:** forward axis projects screen-right, with dominant/near
   side bowl overlapping. Large empty lid-to-rim space, shallow green canopy form and
   the regular thorn-like root spread remain evident from above.
9. **Elevated right, +90°/39°:** forward axis projects screen-left. The dark cavity is
   legible; rounded chamber plus thin ivory ring remains more generic than the hero's
   aggressive heavy-lipped pitcher. Flat leaf blades stand apart from the wood.
10. **Elevated back, 180°/39°:** three chambers, rear keels and complete lid assemblies
    are visible. Bare angular branch forks and the near-radial pointed root skirt remain
    the dominant body structure; no embedded rock cluster or layered root bridge is
    visible at the base.

**Direction caution:** Explorer's printed LEFT says “nose screen left,” while its RIGHT
says “nose screen right.” Source left/right use camera-side naming and have the opposite
screen-facing direction. The generated side images are also approximate and sometimes
three-quarter rather than strict profile. Do not score a label-for-label side overlay
as camera correspondence. World side panels likewise are approximate obliques. Elevated
front/back and physical details remain useful supporting evidence, with the hero as
primary likeness anchor.

Camera source specifies orthographic scale 5, target Blender (0,0,2), 8-unit camera orbit,
600 × 720 output, 24 Cycles samples, denoising off. Those settings explain grain and
framing; no score penalty was assigned simply for sampling noise or absent scenery.

## Integrated corrections, in priority order

1. **Rebuild all three hood/mouth assemblies together.** Enlarge and organically flare
   each lid, with a curved irregular outline, substantial crimson underside, broad
   green-to-red edge and a living fan-shaped throat/hinge flowing into the vessel. Match
   the hero's central hood breadth and diagonal rise. Reduce the detached umbrella/stick
   impression; preserve real open cavities and articulated lid capability.
2. **Sculpt the lip and pitcher as one characterful form.** Replace the uniform torus
   impression and evenly repeated little external teeth with a heavy irregular ivory
   roll, clustered polygonal bulges, several larger tapering front lobes and visible
   magenta inner points. Give the vessel the hero's fuller, tapering asymmetry and woody
   side sheath. Keep central-versus-side size hierarchy and offset side heights.
3. **Rebuild the full trunk/root mass around the hero's negative spaces.** Thicker
   intertwined and bending roots should bridge over embedded gray-purple rocks, with
   fewer repeated straight spikes. Broaden/curve the main branching flow and integrate
   pitcher supports into it. Do not merely attach pebbles to the existing radial skirt.
4. **Restore clusters, not isolated symbols.** Larger curled leaf clusters belong around
   branch junctions and root cavities. Group visible hollow ivory barnacles along the
   branching path and base, with variation in size/orientation. The present small dots
   and separated diamond blades understate this defining coastal growth.
5. **Rebalance facets and palette after structural changes.** Preserve flat-shaded low
   poly and the red/ivory/green/ochre family, but vary facet size/direction with anatomical
   flow. The repeated red bands, enormous simple timber planes and bright regular green
   cap wedges currently feel procedural compared with the hero's denser organic facets.
   Do not use lighting, framing or added scenery to hide the geometry gap.

These are a coordinated complete-character correction, not a request for disconnected
feature studies or repeated camera-only tuning.
