# Tide-Root Colossus V2: independent static likeness review

Reviewed 2026-10-19 UTC by a fresh reviewer who did not author V2.

## Verdict

**3.38 / 5, below the owner's strict >3.5 / 5 target.** This is a whole-model static likeness assessment from the original approved concept, the actual hero render and all nine actual full-frame renders. It is not a single-view score, rounded pass, gameplay assessment or approval to promote the asset.

The identity is readily recognizable: towering antlers, cyan eyes and central crystal, long heavy arms, green growth and stone-root feet all survive the view set. The remaining gap is the character's construction. V2 reads as an orderly wooden robot with a crystal in a rib cage; the original reads as a dense, crooked assembly of roots, bark, stone and overlapping vegetation. The favorable hero angle does not remove the simpler side/back anatomy.

### Silhouette: 3.5

Strong broad shoulders, low hanging hands, separated legs and antler crown establish the correct creature family. However, the reference has irregular interlocking root contours, more varied branching, slanted body masses and a weighty planted stance. V2's paired upper arms and shins are comparatively straight uniform beams; shoulder foliage forms a neat row; the torso is a regular open ring. Its very tall, mostly coplanar antlers dominate the outline. The view set shows these are structural differences rather than a single awkward camera.

### Face: 3.2

The cyan eyes beneath a sloping brow and sharp wooden center are present. The reference has a longer tapering bark-mask face, narrow slit-like eyes in dark sockets, and an integrated crown-to-cheek flow. V2 has a short, wide owl-like mask, relatively large triangular eyes, a distinct protruding central beak and a flat-topped head block. The side views emphasize the beak rather than the elongated mask. This is a recognizable approximation, but the face is a major identity mismatch.

### Characteristic details: 3.2

The crystal, encircling roots, moss/leaf growth, claws and rocky feet are correctly included. The crystal has a particularly clear cyan silhouette. But the details are distributed too tidily: round faceted clumps sit on the shoulders, tiny single clumps mark otherwise bare arm spans, symmetrical knee leaves repeat, and angular stone clumps form near-continuous shoe-like rims. The reference mixes overlapping bark splinters, hanging pointed leaves and gray stone into the limbs and lower body. V2's rib cage resembles a regular segmented frame; its rear is dominated by broad clean panels. The original's back thumbnail supports greater overlapping growth, but does not establish exact hidden geometry.

### Facets / style: 3.7

Broad flat wood planes, clean triangular leaves and the polygonal crystal successfully keep the low-poly toon language. There is no inappropriate smooth realistic material treatment. Yet the consistently straight lumber-like faces and repeated regular foliage solids are less organic than the reference's varied planar breaks. Yellow-green foliage and pale wood look brighter and flatter in these renders; some of that could be lighting, so the structural facet-language differences carry more weight than color alone.

## Full-view observations

- **Hero:** clear identity and crystal hierarchy; good heavy hands. Short owl face, very tall crown, orderly ribs and repeated green shoulder solids remain visible.
- **Front:** broad silhouette is legible; bilateral regularity, open circular rib frame, uniformly spaced fingers and narrow waist make the construction feel assembled rather than grown.
- **Left:** crown collapses to a thin upright profile. Long unbroken upper-arm/shin faces, small projecting face and shallow chest layering reveal the biggest depth/organic-form gaps.
- **Right:** corroborates the left profile; cyan crystal projects visibly, but bark overlap and foliage wrapping are sparse. No assumption of hidden reference-specific detail is needed to see this simplification.
- **Back:** broad V-shaped plank panels and central straight spine dominate; leaf coverage is concentrated along the top. Much less root/leaf interleaving than the concept's rear thumbnail suggests.
- **Top:** confirms that the antler crown is mostly in one plane and shoulder clumps are arranged in a tidy band. This view is used for structural coherence, not exact top-view matching to an unavailable reference.
- **Diagonal front:** heavy arms and cyan center remain strong; the symmetric knee-leaf ornaments, simple hip wedge and straight leg construction are exposed.
- **Diagonal left:** the crystal reads well in depth, while the open ring/frame construction, short face and sparse side growth remain apparent.
- **Diagonal right:** coherent counterpart to diagonal left; long planar limbs and stacked stone-foot solids are still conspicuous, not repaired by the better-lit surface.
- **Diagonal back:** clean contiguous broad rear panels again dominate. This is the clearest oblique evidence that the model needs full-volume organic rebuilding, rather than only front decoration.

No entire back or top shape is treated as precisely recoverable from the concept. The approved sheet's large character, action depiction and small turnaround-like thumbnails guide identity; all are concept art, not an orthographic production specification.

## Highest-impact complete correction

**Replace the tidy beam-and-ring body with a coherent interlocking root anatomy, while rebuilding the face as its integrated crown centerpiece.** Treat this as one complete volume revision, not a few additional decorative leaves:

1. Recompose shoulders, chest, back, forearms and calves from a small number of irregular tapered, twisting root masses. Break parallel edges and repeated segmentation; let major roots overlap and diverge into claws and stone-root feet. Preserve the recognizable broad shoulders and long heavy hands.
2. Replace the regular chest ring with staggered asymmetric bark/root ribs that cradle the crystal. Carry those root flows around the flanks and over the back so side/rear views have designed layered anatomy rather than broad blank boards.
3. Lengthen the face downward into the narrow angular bark mask; reduce eye apertures, deepen their dark recesses and make the brow/crown roots flow into the cheeks. Avoid the current separate owl mask/beak read. Give the antler branches varied angles and some real fore-aft divergence rather than merely adding more coplanar tines.
4. Integrate pointed leaf clusters and irregular moss masses at overlaps across shoulders, back, elbows, hips and shins. Vary size and direction; avoid repeated rows of green balls. Embed gray stone chunks selectively in the lower roots instead of a regular shoe rim.
5. Re-render the unchanged canonical nine cameras and hero, then conduct a fresh complete score. A nicer hero alone is insufficient. Do not promise that this prescription will produce a particular score.
