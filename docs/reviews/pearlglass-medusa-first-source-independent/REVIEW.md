## Complete-character corrections, in priority order

1. **Rebuild the bell volume and rim.** The approved hero has a thick, irregular, lobed umbrella with a rounded, undulating crown and substantial cyan underside chambers. The source reads as a shallow conical sun umbrella: straight radial ridges converge at a single hub, its outer edge forms repeated pointed scallops, and its side profile is thin. Give the dome uneven broad rounded lobes, a thick rolled/scalloped rim and a deeper integrated underside. Keep the whole ribbon-to-bell proportion visible while correcting this; increasing tessellation alone will not solve it.
2. **Reconstruct the orange focal organ as one clustered form.** The source's large orange angular lobes and long downward spikes resemble hanging crystals or teeth. The hero has a luminous central nugget inside a dense, irregular rosette of smaller orange protrusions and short curling appendages. Make the center compact, rounder and more strongly differentiated from its surrounding cluster, with shorter varied lobes. Keep ribbon roots from covering the most important front-facing orange center. Do not add eyes or a mouth.
3. **Integrate the ivory ribs into underside anatomy.** Current ribs read as separate straight or kinked bars laid radially under the canopy, with conspicuous pale ends protruding at the rim. The hero's ribs are thick, curved, tapering organic arches separating recessed teal pockets. Bend, merge and seat them into the bell volume, taper at the core and avoid isolated uniform rod ends. This is a structural correction, not just a color adjustment.
4. **Reshape the entire ribbon set in depth.** Source ribbons have broad flat panels, abrupt zigzag bends and long spear-like tips. The hero's ribbons have continuous twisting, folded, unevenly thick sculptural bodies, more expressive S curves and curled/tapered ends. Add gradual twist, convex/concave cross-sections and smoother changes in width while retaining low-poly facets. Organize overlapping ribbons to leave deliberate negative spaces instead of flat curtain overlaps; preserve varied lengths and a few narrower inner ribbons.
5. **Recompose facets and palette across the whole body.** The cap's long radial sliver triangles and sector-like color bands make a pinwheel, unlike the hero's irregular, roughly chunky triangular patches. Reduce the hub convergence impression, vary triangle direction and scale, and distribute cream/mint/rose across adjacent lobes rather than broad radial regions. Ribbons also need less planar color striping. Retain faceting, but use it to describe curved volumes. Some cream highlights clip toward white and shaded rear ribbons become muddy; confirm materials under controlled comparable lighting after geometry improves. Static grain/lighting prevents a precise intrinsic roughness or translucency diagnosis.
6. **Retain and refine barnacle detail in the corrected shapes.** Current clusters repeat small similar groupings at comparable ribbon intervals and read as thin protruding posts from side/back. The hero has irregular, fuller, clustered hollow cups with varied size and orientation. Give clusters more grounded attachment and depth, preserve holes, and vary grouping and placement to follow folds. Keep their scale subordinate to the bell/core/ribbon silhouette rather than adding more as compensation for larger shape errors.
