## Verdict — 2026-10-19, 02:06 UTC

**Retain provisionally for a matched flat-color check.** The actual-depth hood
correction restores a thicker diagonal cover and a stronger angry read than
the preceding compact, constant-depth version. The long crownward horns remain
gone. The model keeps its pouch fullness and eye placement in these views.

Compared with the supported-surface fallback, the upper wedge still reads
broader and more rectangular, with a sharp temple transition. This does not
justify another geometry guess from neutral clay alone. Restore the original
palette on this exact frozen geometry and the fallback, under matched cameras,
before deciding whether the brow is a short orange wedge or a broad visor.
Keep the mouth unchanged for that comparison so its influence is isolated.

No full-model score, finished-face acceptance, owner approval or runtime claim
is made. The supported-surface remains the visual fallback until that check.

## Matched native-palette comparison — 2026-10-19, 02:11 UTC

**Bounded retain of the connected baselinehood for mouth work.** Color makes
the short orange hood legible and the angry read survives. The hero's raised
separate far-brow wing is reduced; the side no longer has the old block cap.
This resolves enough of the clay ambiguity to continue the connected face
branch without another brow-geometry pass now.

The remaining concern is the radiating temple/under-eye transition: broad
brown shadow triangles and interpolated teal/orange smears make the socket
look more excavated than the approved reference. The brows still have fairly
straight upper edges, but the colored result does not read as the earlier
large crownward visor. Keep this geometry for the next branch, then author
clean flat color-region boundaries and recheck the dark triangles. If they
remain with clean color regions, assess the cavity corners geometrically.
