- **Silhouette:** The branching crown, broad shoulders, long hanging arms,
  open root-rib torso, narrow hips and splayed stone-root feet make the intended
  creature immediately recognizable. The large limbs have substantial volume
  rather than being a flat facade. However, pure side views are markedly narrow;
  the torso and shoulder mass read more like a thin upright framework than the
  original's hunched, layered tree body. Bilateral regularity and straight shin
  sections keep a constructed wooden-humanoid quality.
- **Face:** Cyan slanted eyes, a severe angular brow, long pointed wooden mask
  and branching antlers preserve the central identity well in front and both
  forward diagonals. The bright central forehead triangle and very straight
  pointed muzzle simplify the original's more irregular nested bark mask.
  Antler geometry is convincing in outline but unusually orderly and planar.
- **Details:** Cyan faceted core, enclosing root ribs, joint foliage, stone toes
  and separate hooked fingers are all present and legible. The greenery is
  predominantly thin, sharp, isolated blades with repeated joint-band placement;
  the reference has fuller clustered moss/leaf volumes draped through its body.
  Stone is concentrated into chunky boot-like feet, with much less dark stone
  woven through the legs and torso. Broad bare limb surfaces lack the reference's
  interlocking wood, stone and vegetation complexity. The core is recognizable
  but has fewer large facet changes and a more uniformly bright upper face.
- **Style:** Broad flat-shaded planes and the wood/green/cyan/gray palette fit
  the approved low-poly direction. Major forms are coherent rather than smooth
  generic tubes. Repetitive angular limb blocks, thin leaf triangles and hard
  pale stone blocks remain less naturally composed than the original. Render
  noise and lighting differences were not treated as intrinsic mesh defects.

## Full-nine observations

1. **Front:** Strong crown/face/core hierarchy and long-arm silhouette. Repeated
   limb segment rhythm and sparse chest greenery are conspicuous.
2. **Left:** Real finger, arm and torso depth is visible, but the body is thin
   and upright; the hand and arm merge into one long side silhouette.
3. **Right:** Confirms the same narrow torso and regular limb construction;
   isolated foliage tufts do not build a substantial organic shoulder canopy.
4. **Back:** The root-rib construction and shoulder greenery continue around the
   model. Large exposed back and leg blocks remain comparatively plain, with a
   small cyan glimpse through the upper back structure.
5. **Top:** Confirms crown, shoulders and foliage have spatial arrangement;
   leaf blades appear as a scattered angular layer rather than thick moss crowns.
6. **Diagonal front:** Best combined face/core/arm read, with the long mask
   integrated into the rib opening. Feet look like separate gray caps.
7. **Diagonal left:** Face remains recognizable in profile; chest root curves
   retain volume. Forearm blockiness and attached leaf tufts are easy to see.
8. **Diagonal right:** Strong overall recognition survives the opposite view;
   plain shin planes and simplified stone integration remain obvious.
9. **Diagonal back:** Back-root volumes and foliage survive the walk-around
   evidence, but the clean segmented legs and orderly shoulder shapes still
   depart from the reference's tangled, grown construction.

## Residual priorities

The static threshold is met; do not describe this as an exact concept match.
The highest-value remaining changes, if further likeness work is requested,
are fuller clustered foliage, more intertwined wood/stone limb construction,
less boot-like feet, and a deeper, slightly hunched torso profile. Keep the
successful face, antler silhouette, cyan core and long-arm proportions intact.
