## Actual nine-view findings

- Front: repeated green shoulder caps and elbow/hip/shin cuffs, broad barrel cage, squat leg rhythm and parallel digit rows. Face is legible but has a broad bright triangular forehead and continuous beak-like chin; core reads as a few large slabs.
- Left: canopy creates a flat overhanging roof; oversized forearm hides the separate winding root paths. Crown reads as closely packed forks.
- Right: the same roof/cuff rhythm persists, with a nearly continuous thick arm mass and abrupt green bands rather than growth following wood.
- Back: stacked similar moss lobes become a scalloped cape. Lower back/thigh transitions expose large plain root blocks; foliage distribution changes abruptly at the cape edge.
- Top: repeated lobe construction is most obvious: an almost unbroken broad cap instead of unequal depth-separated growth islands.
- Elevated front: oversized shoulder lobes compress the head/chest silhouette; uniform triangular hanging points dominate the root anatomy.
- Elevated left: nearer shoulder and rib greenery crowd the cyan heart; three parallel fingers read as standardized bars.
- Elevated right: long pale face survives, but bright broad leaf planes and cuffs dominate; chest clasp is chunky relative to the exposed faceted core.
- Elevated back: canopy roof and repeated hanging perimeter remain conspicuous in depth; leg rooting is less sinuous than the original.

Largest consequential mismatch: **the whole body's repeated cap-and-cuff rhythm over a broad, compressed wood cage**, rather than the original's branching, staggered living anatomy. Merely shrinking leaves recreates pass2/3's sparse patches; merely adding lobes recreates the failed tents. This needs a coupled body/foliage reconstruction, not another isolated foliage patch.
