## Verdict — 2026-10-19, 01:15 UTC

**None of A, B or C should advance to detailed modeling in its present shape.**

width or height does not restore the approved character's rounded pouch,
hooded angry expression or compact fin-supported silhouette.

V71 remains materially closer to the approved original in core roundness and
facial identity. This comparison does **not** penalize the blockouts for their
deliberately missing spikes, tail, toe detail, textures or final lighting.

runtime result or owner acceptance. The established V71 review is unchanged.
Variant labels and builder concerns were supplied in the brief; this is an

## Findings by study

### A

The middle width/height choice does not resolve the shared construction. The
cheeks form a broad rectangular slab, the mouth is a hole through that slab,
and the cream region ends along a nearly straight horizontal ledge above the
tapered lower mass. In side view, the rear wall, long straight roof and belt-like
planes give the core a box profile. The inclined fin supports remain long.

### B

The flattest, widest body exaggerates the slab and level eye band. Its broader
foot spread compounds the flat, wide impression rather than creating the
reference's rounded bulk. The lower stance is a variable worth studying, but
these proportions are not a winning anatomy to retain wholesale.

### C

The taller core supplies more upper-body volume, but it remains a taller version
of the same rectangular front and box profile. The nearly round exposed eyes
and hexagonal mouth read more surprised or neutral than heavily hooded and
angry. A higher crown alone does not create the required pouch or cheek shape.

### Shared failure

The clay views establish that the problem is geometric. Flat color then adds a
level orange visor and straight cream belt, making the slab even more explicit.
The three options vary proportions without testing substantially different
front-half anatomy. None is selected as the base for detail work.

## Retain the useful ideas, not these dimensions

- Continuous socket-to-cheek construction remains a sensible approach. It must
  be sculpted to the reference's expression rather than treated as proof of it.
- Webbed fin fans remain the right general vocabulary, but the present long,
  inclined supports do not yet establish the requested short, weight-bearing
  stance.
- Neutral clay plus flat color is effective evidence. Keep these simple views
  until the core, facial identity and stance are right.
- Preserve V71 as the silhouette and identity comparison. Reusing its successful
  visible relationships does not require inheriting its previous topology.

## Required anatomical corrections before the next detail pass

### 1. Replace the full-width cheek slab with a rounded pouch

Build a forward-projecting cream volume below the mouth. Bring the lateral cheek
corners backward and inward relative to that central pouch. Replace the planar
cross-face cheek row and horizontal cutoff with connected, convex cheek and
lower-pouch transitions. Keep the low-poly style through a few intentional
planes, not through a rectangular extrusion.

**Acceptance check:** in clay hero and side views, the front half must read as
a rounded inflated volume with a forward bulge below the mouth. It must not read
as a flat mask over a tapered chin. In front view, the lateral contour should
turn around the volume rather than form long vertical slab edges and a straight
cross-face ledge.

### 2. Put the angry expression into the eyelid/orbital geometry

Sculpt paired upper orbital ridges that descend inward toward the nose, with
substantial upper-eye occlusion and a clear inner-corner pinch. Preserve visible
gold irises and pupils. Stop using a level row of nearly circular holes beneath
a horizontal orange band as the facial layout.

**Acceptance check:** the clay front and hero must already read as hooded and
angry before orange paint or separate brow decoration is added. The two eye
openings need shaped upper lids and cheek support rather than round holes punched
into a slab. Both profiles must show that the orbital structure belongs to the
core, without a rear tab or rectangular projecting cap.

### 3. Restore a shaped beak-to-mouth-to-pouch transition

Use a raised central bill/bridge and a distinct lower-lip transition into the
pouch. The dark opening must belong to this anatomy, not appear as a hexagonal
hole cut through the cream face. V71 and the original are useful contour checks;
neither requires copying an exact hidden cavity.

**Acceptance check:** in clay hero and side, identify the upper bill, opening,
lower lip and pouch as connected forms. The mouth should support the grumpy gape
instead of a surprised punched-hole expression. Do not solve this with a darker
texture or by uniformly scaling the opening.

### 4. Round the core profile before adding facet detail

Replace the long straight roof and flat rear wall with an intentionally rounded
core outline. Use oblique planes to turn around the mass, and avoid a belt
structure that simply makes a box look more segmented. The higher crown in C
and lower stance in B are parameters to explore, not successful shapes already
chosen.

**Acceptance check:** compare clay hero, front and side against V71's basic
rounded core and the approved illustration. A new cage should retain that
roundness while improving the pouch and facial integration. It should not need
spikes or color to disguise a box silhouette.

### 5. Shorten and broaden the functional fin supports

Reduce the visible long inclined leg-like section and concentrate the webbed
fan near the planted end. Keep a believable broad connection to the body and a
stable stance. Avoid solving body height by spreading thin supports farther out.

**Acceptance check:** front and hero show a compact supported body, not a block
on long flared stilts. Side view still shows usable clearance and a continuous
fin attachment. Toe detail is unnecessary for passing this shape check.

## Next comparison method

Make genuinely different anatomical blockouts, not further width/height variants
of the rejected slab. Each should solve the five corrections before detailing.
Keep cameras, lighting and presentation consistent across alternatives; show
clay and flat-color hero/front/side for each. State any intentional change of
scale or pose, rather than hiding it in framing.

Judge the pouch, angry orbital structure and compact stance first. If all three
fail, reject all three again. Do not select the least weak option just to advance
the schedule. Move to the full nine-view and detail stages only after a coherent
whole-character direction is demonstrated.

The concept's camera differs from these actual views, and its tiny alternate
views do not encode exact hidden anatomy. Use them as directional constraints,
not a source of invented millimeter targets. No exact 3D ratio or eventual score
is promised by this review.

### What improved, and what still fails

Wrapping the cheek corners removes the worst rectangular slab. The sloped
socket frame is a better expression direction than the first level eye row.
Those are useful construction lessons.

However, the side still shows broad belt planes and a long, nearly straight
bevel from the lower mouth/lip region back toward the underside. The hero's
lower mass tapers into a pointed chin. This reads as a solid blunt head with a
receding underside, rather than an inflated forward pouch. The long inclined
fin supports remain. It is rounder than A/B/C without yet restoring the more
successful body and facial relationships already present in V71.

### Concrete landmark relationships for the next pass

1. **Start with V71's spatial landmarks.** Initially retain its crown/rear
   outline, eye centers, bill tip, mouth corners and lower lip while replacing
   surface connectivity. These are starting guards, not permanently locked
   anatomy: move a landmark later only for an explicit reference-supported
   correction. New topology alone is not a reason to move every successful
   relationship at once.
2. **Give the pouch an intermediate forward bulge below the mouth.** Between
   lower lip and pouch bottom, add a full convex cross-section before turning
   gently back into the underside. The reference's cream belly reads as a
   substantial lobe beneath the gape. Do not connect lip to underside with one
   long diagonal bevel or make the low point a central spike-like chin.
3. **Separate cheek return from pouch fullness.** Let the lateral cheeks wrap
   behind the mouth corners while retaining the central lower-front volume.
   Pulling the entire facial row backward defeats the pouch projection that the
   wrap was intended to support.
4. **Preserve eye placement before changing socket shape.** Use V71's eye-center
   and bill/eye relationships in the same hero/front cameras, then sculpt the
   inward-descending upper orbital ridge. Integration must not casually push the
   eyes around onto the sides of the head or change their relative visibility.
5. **Treat fin shortening as its own correction after the core passes.** Reduce
   root-to-ground reach and develop a broad planted web; do not simultaneously
   move the belly, raise the body and spread the feet to hide a changed support
   arrangement.

The relationships above do not claim an exact unseen side anatomy from the
concept. No absolute millimeter target or inferred hidden curvature is imposed.

### Efficient next check

Make a matched neutral-clay, core-only V71 hero/front/side baseline before
judging the retopology. Explicitly label this limited comparison and use equal
detail scope in the new cage. Keep the same cameras and lighting. First show
that replacing topology preserves V71's recognizable core and face; then test
the forward pouch correction independently. Do not add detailed paint, spikes
or fine facets to rescue a weaker core.
