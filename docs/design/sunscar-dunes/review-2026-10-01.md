## 1. The verdict in five lines

1. **The light is aimed the wrong way.** The key light is a 5° sun straight ahead of the spawn view. Everything you look
   at is back-lit, and with no shadows, no rim and a weak fill, it is a black cut-out on a flat brown floor.
   The mockup's light grazes the dunes from the side and splits every face into glowing orange and cool blue.
2. **The dunes are 5 m washboard stripes, not dunes.** The mockup has 10–30 m crescent dunes with knife-edge crests that
   fill two thirds of the frame. The game has parallel 4–6 m ridges every 50 m, and its horizon sits at mid-screen.
3. **The sand is untextured vertex colour.** There is no texture, no normal detail, no ripple shadow and no sheen. It
   is half the brightness of the mockup's sand and a third of its variation.
4. **The quest has no presentation at all.** No start, no objective chip, no marker on the minimap or in the world,
   nothing on the map, and the boss is not a step. The engine's quest presentation (chip, markers, NPC talk, map card)
   exists, and Driftwood, Pine Hollow and Nalati all use it. Signal Dunes calls none of it.
5. **Every hero object is unreadable:** the fires are a few pixels, the ray and the Matriarch are flat black kites, the
   storm is a tan fog, and the whip coil covers the middle of the screen.

## 2. Why it looks bad and feels half-done, ranked

Ranked by how much of Jake's "nothing like the mockup" each one explains, and by how many frames it spoils.

### R1 · Dusk lighting: the key light is behind everything you look at (every frame)

- **What you see** (`evidence-1`, `evidence-2`): the caravan, the well, the strider, the tower and the ray are
  near-black silhouettes. The sand facing the camera is one dark brown, (56, 33, 28) sRGB mean in the near field,
  against (116, 67, 39) in the mockup. Its spread is σ ≈ 10 against σ ≈ 28: half as bright, a third as varied.
- **Why:**
  - `look/render.ts` `KEY.dir = (-0.55, 0.09, -0.83)`: the light comes from a sun 5° up, ahead-left of the spawn view.
    The spawn faces −Z, the tower faces −Z, and the trails run north. So for most of the walk the player looks into the
    light and sees the unlit side of every face.
  - `manifest.sky.sun` is `{ azimuth: 285, elevation: 4 }`.
  - The fill is a hemisphere light, `0x5a5c9a` over `0x6a3420` at 0.9, plus `envIntensity` 0.35: it lifts the shadows
    to a muddy brown-violet.
  - There are no shadows: the terrain's `receiveShadow = false`, so a dune never shadows the next one.
  - The look has no rim or back light.
  - The previous agent's emissive fix (`0x2a140a` on the whip) only papers over this on the viewmodel.
- **What good looks like:**
  - The mockup and every Pine Hollow frame keep a low warm key across or behind the camera, never in its face.
  - Lit faces read warm orange, shade faces read cool blue-violet (never black), and the crest line between them is the
    strongest edge in the frame.
  - At dusk the afterglow is where the sky is bright, not where the light comes from. Art-direct the two apart.
    - Keep the band behind the tower.
    - Put the key 70–110° off the spawn view axis, 8–12° up.
    - Give the fill a cool sky colour so the shadow sides read blue.
    - Turn on terrain shadows (receive) on the desktop tier, and bake dune self-shadow into vertex AO on the phone tier.
    - Add a warm rim term for creatures and props, so a silhouette keeps an orange edge.

### R2 · Dune scale, silhouettes and composition (the spawn frame, every wide shot)

- **What you see:**
  - The horizon sits at ~50 % of the screen; the mockup's is at ~34 %.
  - Below it, three thin dark stripes run parallel to the screen (`evidence-1` A), then a flat brown floor.
  - There is no big form, no leading line to the tower and no foreground interest.
- **Why:**
  - `world/dunes.ts`: `WAVE = 50`, amplitude `4.2 + 1.6·n`, ridges laid across Z.
  - Every crest is parallel to the spawn view, so the dunes stack as horizontal bands.
  - The 32° walkability cap was met by keeping the dunes small. Making them big with gentle trails was the other way.
- **What good looks like:**
  - Big crescent (barchan) and star-dune forms, 10–30 m tall, crests angled across the view so they lead the eye to the
    tower crest.
  - A sharp slip face on the lee, 30–34° (Jake's climb limit is 40°).
  - The walk graded in along the trails, the way Pine Hollow grades its paths (`TerrainSpec.graded`), instead of the
    whole field being flattened.
  - The spawn crest high enough that the first frame looks down over two or three dune rows to the tower, which the
    mockup does.

### R3 · Materials: the sand, the ripples and every prop are vertex colour (every frame)

- **What you see:**
  - Smooth brown plastic: no grain, no ripple relief, no specular sheen on the wind-packed faces.
  - The ripple patch (`sandRipple`, ±11 % albedo, fading 25–140 m) does not show at phone resolution.
  - Albedo stripes are not ripples: real ripples are light and shadow, a normal effect.
  - The yardangs and boulders are flat-shaded instanced boxes.
- **Why:**
  - `terrainPainter` builds a 192² plane over 480 m: a 2.5 m vertex grid, vertex colour only.
  - `MeshStandardMaterial` is at roughness 0.96, with no maps.
  - The props are code primitives or 700–2400-tri Hunyuan turbo meshes with baked paint at a low texel density.
  - Pine Hollow ships tiled PBR ground textures. Nalati paints tileable textures. Signal Dunes ships none.
- **What good looks like:**
  - A tileable sand albedo, normal and roughness set: two scales (fine grain and 8–15 cm ripples), triplanar or
    world-UV, with a third "wind ripple" normal oriented by the dune's lee direction. Generated once, KTX2, a half-res
    phone tier.
  - Macro variation: darker, coarser trough sand; pale, fine crest sand; wind-streak lines on the stoss faces.
  - A faint anisotropic sheen on the lit faces.

### R4 · The quest has no presentation (Jake's "look at the Driftwood quest")

- **What you see** (`evidence-1` A vs C/D):
  - On arrival nothing names a goal.
  - There is no chip under the minimap. Driftwood's is "◆ WHO LIT THE FIRE? │ CASTAWAY 123 M ▲" from frame one.
  - No icon on the minimap, no marker in the world, no "DISCOVERED · …" toasts, no map card.
  - The first step, "Find the caravan's logbook", is never shown anywhere. The caravan is 89 m away and 62° to the left
    of where the spawn faces.
  - The instructions live in one transient toast (the logbook text).
  - Each brazier needs walk → pour → crack with nothing marking it.
  - When the fire is lit, "Something vast stirs in the basin" toasts, but the basin is unmarked and nothing happens
    (R7).
  - The quest then completes and pays 5 coins before the boss, so the boss is an afterthought, not the finale.
- **Why:**
  - `quest/install.ts` is a bare `QuestState` with four steps and no `intro`, `startWhen`, `chip`, `hint` or `markers`.
  - Nothing wires `QuestChip`, `fullMap.setQuest`, `minimap.setMarks`, an `NpcTalk`, `manifest.pois` or
    `manifest.minimap`.
  - The engine has all of it: `QuestMarker` / `QuestStep.markers` / `intro` in `src/engine/quest/core.ts`,
    `QuestChip` / `NpcTalk` in `src/engine/quest/view.ts`, `Minimap.setMarks`, `Map` quest diamonds.
  - Driftwood (`quest/Spine.ts`, `questLine.ts`), Pine Hollow (`quest/index.ts:447`, `wardensHollow.ts`) and Nalati
    each wire it by hand.
  - **Engine / docs gap:** `docs/SHARDS.md`'s quests row documents only
    `new QuestState({ id, title, completeFlag, steps }, …)`. A clean-room builder working from the docs alone (E357
    Z3, this shard's origin) cannot know the chip, the markers, the map card, the NPC talk or the minimap marks exist.
- **What good looks like** (Driftwood, Pine Hollow):
  - A start that names the goal in the first 10 seconds: an NPC to talk to, or a found object at the spawn.
  - Every step carries a marker. The chip shows the step, the distance and the bearing. The minimap shows the
    diamonds. The map tab shows the quest card with its hint. Places toast "DISCOVERED · …" on arrival.
  - A reward beat at the end, the boss included.
  - See mockup B.

### R5 · Effects: the fires and the storm (the quest's payoffs)

- **What you see** (`evidence-3`):
  - A lit waymark at 20 m is a ~12 px orange cone. At 40 m it is gone. The signal fire at 75 m is ~3 px.
  - Nothing marks a brazier from afar: no smoke, no glow on the sand, no light that carries.
  - The storm phase (`evidence-4` E) tints the whole frame flat tan. There are no streaks, no gusts and no
    silhouette loss, so it reads as a fog colour change, not a sand storm.
- **Why:**
  - The fire is a cone plus an additive falloff sphere (`world/places.ts`).
  - There is no particle system for flames, embers or smoke anywhere in the engine, game or kit. The kit's `Particles`
    is dust motes, mist and fireflies, and its dust fades out at dusk.
  - The storm is `Fog` near/far plus two `BackSide` sphere shells.
- **What good looks like:**
  - Mockup C: a 1.5 m layered fire with embers blown downwind, a smoke column into the sky and a warm light pool on
    the sand.
  - Each lit waymark is visible from the next as a glow plus smoke, so the chain itself is the guidance.
  - The signal fire is the brightest thing in the level: a 3–4 m flame and a column that is visible from the spawn.
  - The storm: blown-sand streak sheets crossing the view, a ground-hugging drift, the band dimmed, gusts that pulse
    the fog.

### R6 · Creatures and the Matriarch: flat black kites (every combat frame)

- **What you see:**
  - The dune ray and the Matriarch are a few flat triangles. They read as a black paper kite from below
    (`evidence-4` C/D: at 9 s she is a black slab across the top half of the screen).
  - The mockup's ray is a pterosaur-like glider with articulated wings, a head and a tail.
  - The strider is a good camel-like Hunyuan model, but black when back-lit (`evidence-2` D).
  - The skitterers are barely visible.
- **Why:**
  - `species/duneRay.ts` is a code model on a five-bone rig.
  - The Matriarch is the same mesh at 3.6×, so its low tri count and flat shading grow with it.
  - C6 generated only the strider among the creatures.
- **What good looks like:**
  - A generated, textured ray (TRELLIS.2 or Hunyuan3D, the better of the two) with a pale belly, mottled back, wing
    membranes and a tail.
  - Rigged to the five bones, with a wing-beat cycle and a glide.
  - The Matriarch is her own model: scars, a crest of fin spines, a darker back.
  - Under the R1 rim light both keep an orange edge against the sky.

### R7 · The Matriarch's entrance does not happen where the player is (the climax)

- **What you see:**
  - From the tower deck or the bowl's rim, lighting the fire gives a toast and nothing else (`evidence-4` A: 16 s,
    empty sky).
  - She rises only once the player walks inside `BASIN.r − 6` (50 m) of the bowl's centre, which nothing tells them to
    do.
  - Inside, the rise is a black kite climbing behind the title card (B), then a black slab overhead (D).
- **Why:**
  - `combat/matriarch.ts` `inArena` gates the `BossBrain` intro.
  - The basin is 75 m north of the tower, behind the tower from the approach.
  - This explains the 21:50 Handoff's "the Matriarch didn't come into view". Note also that `animals.calm` holds her
    buried, and that `polcap.mjs`'s `basin` pose faced +Z, away from the bowl.
- **What good looks like:**
  - The fire's light is the summons. From the tower deck the player sees the bowl erupt, a sand fountain and a column
    of dust.
  - She circles the tower once, back-lit by the band with an orange rim, then the quest step "Face the Matriarch"
    marks the bowl.
  - The boss's death is the quest's end and its reward.

### R8 · First-person hands and the whip (every frame)

- **What you see** (`evidence-5`):
  - The idle coil is a ~35 %-of-screen pale ring through the middle and right of the frame. It covers the crosshair
    region's lower half and reads as a hose or a rope, not a whip.
  - The glove is a few flat-shaded boxes.
  - The crack is a straight, thick, uniform plank from the hand to the crosshair: no taper, no curl, no blur, no tip
    flash, no dust puff.
- **Why:** `weapons/whipModel.ts` (restored at 597c3b7b): `TubeGeometry` coil, primitive glove, a vertex-colour braid.
  The coil sits at (0.09, −0.17, −0.42), in front of the face rather than at the hip.
- **What good looks like:**
  - The mockup and mockup D: a dark leather glove entering from the lower right corner, holding a short braided handle.
  - A loose coil a third the size, hanging down to the bottom-right edge.
  - The centre of the screen clear.
  - The lash on a crack: a curling, tapering line, with a bright tip snap, a puff of sand at the impact and a 2–3-frame
    motion smear.
  - The brief is in §6.

### R9 · Palette and the sky band (every frame)

- **What you see:**
  - The sky takes about half the frame. Its band is a thin orange line hugging the dunes, and above it is a clean
    saturated blue-violet: (29, 32, 71) against the mockup's greyer (50, 49, 55).
  - There are no clouds, so the sky has no texture and no light catching it.
  - Fog `0x7a4656` violet-pink washes the mid dunes pink: the mid-frame mean is (146, 99, 102) against the mockup's
    (88, 51, 36).
- **Why:**
  - `look/sky.ts`: the band lives in `h < 0.07`; mauve to violet by 0.22, indigo by 0.62.
  - `clouds: false`.
  - The grade is neutral by design ("contrast clips the indigo zenith"). That was a symptom of the dome being too
    saturated, not a reason to skip a grade.
- **What good looks like:**
  - A band two to three times taller behind the tower.
  - Thin under-lit cloud streaks (pink-orange bellies) on the band side.
  - A desaturated violet-grey upper sky.
  - Aerial perspective that cools and lightens the far dune rows into violet layers instead of pink.
  - A light grade: warm highlights, blue-violet shadows. Fit the LUT in LOOK-LOOP step 6.

### R10 · Model quality and density (every place)

- **What you see:**
  - Each place is one object on empty sand.
  - The caravan is a tipped wagon with a code canvas. The well is a ring and posts. The braziers are a post and a bowl.
  - Between the places there are 10 yardangs, a few boulders and scrub, at a 400 m scale. The frame has nothing every
    few metres; Nalati's look doc names that as lever 6.
- **What good looks like:**
  - Each place is a small vignette with a story:
    - the caravan: spilled crates, a broken wheel, drag marks, a lantern;
    - the well: a stone apron, a trough, a dead tamarisk;
    - the waymarks: cairns or posts, half-buried.
  - The trails are marked by cairns or old posts, which is also guidance.
  - Mid-scale dressing everywhere: wind-cut outcrops, dead acacia, bleached bones, tracks.

### R11 · HUD use (every frame)

- **What you see:**
  - The minimap is a green blob: the default palette, not sand, with no trails, no places and no quest icons
    (`evidence-1` A vs C).
  - There is no objective chip (R4) and no "DISCOVERED" toasts (no `manifest.pois`).
  - The toast stack is the only quest text.
  - No LOCK chip appears in any frame, even with a strider 9 m ahead or the grounded Matriarch 16 m ahead. Driftwood's
    crab at 7 m shows LOCK (`dw-crab`).
- **Why:**
  - `manifest.ts` has no `minimap` (Driftwood's has `openWater`, `paths`, `pieces`), no `pois`, and no
    `fullMap.setQuest`.
  - Lock-on for the ray is the engine builder's job (sol-lock, a `flight.lockRange` field already exists in
    `src/engine/ai/flight.ts`).
  - The missing LOCK on the ground strider needs checking once that lands: it is either the same gate or the whip's
    lane.
- **What good looks like:** a sand-palette minimap with the trails and the places, quest diamonds, a chip and LOCK when
  a target is in range. Baseline HUD only; nothing new on screen.

### R12 · Title and Explore cards

- **What you see** (`evidence-5` D): one flat SVG (a gradient, three bezier dunes, a four-line tower, a dot), used for
  the thumb, the portrait, the landscape and all four Explore tabs.
- **What good looks like:**
  - Hero images captured from the finished hero scenes and graded, as in `art/hero-images/round-4-pine-hollow-in-engine/`.
  - The Explore cards cut from the same frames.
  - A loading card from H1.
  - This is P5, after the look loop.

### R13 · The first minute

- Pack A (`PACKS[0]`, −40, 40) is 42 m from the spawn on the path to the caravan, and the ray glides over the spawn.
  The captures before this one had a damage vignette in most frames.
- The first minute should be the view, the goal and one readable creature at a distance, not a bite from an unseen
  skitterer.
