## Heavy GPU lane (2026-09-25, the structural GPU work; the fix lane keeps density + the tier knobs)

**The ruler: `scripts/pine-hollow-gpu.mjs`** (serve a clean export with `vite preview`; one muted Metal Chromium). The iPhone
16 Pro's real screen (402×874 @3), Pine Hollow paused at each pose, the frozen frame drawn by the tool:

- **GPU ms/frame** = 12 frames back to back, then a real GPU sync (a fresh 1-px write + readPixels), ÷ 12; the 20th percentile
  of 24 rounds (other GPU clients on the box only ever add time). Stable to ~2 %, scales with the pixel count.
  EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2 exists on ANGLE Metal but over-reads 2× whole and 10× once cut per pass, and gl.finish()
  does not wait for the GPU at all — so no per-pass timeline: per-pass costs are **subtractions** (the frame with that pass
  or group switched off, base and toggled rounds alternating; `null` = the A/B's own noise floor, ±0.08 ms).
- **Overdraw** = every material swapped to add 1/255 per fragment, no depth test (R opaque, G alpha-tested, B transparent);
  `raster` = alpha test off, i.e. every fragment the GPU shades before it can discard.
- Measured at pixel ratio 3 (1206×2622, `--scale=3`): at the game's 2 the M5 is within ~0.5 ms of its CPU submit and the
  subtractions saturate. The game's own pixel ratio 2 (804×1748) reads 1.9–2.2 ms.

What it says: the frame is spread wide — alpha-tested foliage 20–35 % (the needle cards most of it; a crown is 16–32+
card layers deep), **the terrain's splat shader 17–31 %** (one full-screen layer, but a dear one), the shadow cascade
~20 %, the colour chain ~15 % (bloom + god rays; the volumetric march is only ~0.05 ms = 2 %, cheap to keep), the sky
5–15 %, the depth copy ~5 %. No single switch halves it; the levers below stack.

**Calibration (low confidence).** Jake's 71 ms at the stones against ~1.9 ms here at the game's own 804×1748 is ~37×; the
A18 Pro's GPU is ~7–9× narrower than the M5 Max's (cores × clock, bandwidth). So the WebGL frame alone should be ~15–20 ms
on his phone: the rest of the 71 ms is likely not this GPU work — iOS compositing (the HUD's ~15 `backdrop-filter: blur`
panels and discs re-blurred over a canvas that changes every frame), the main thread, thermal (charging). Dynamic resolution
(below) adapts to whatever the phone can do; the HUD blur is flagged for a phone A/B.

while charging; the Ghost Stag event was also up in his photo. Only the phone can say which: the re-check above.
