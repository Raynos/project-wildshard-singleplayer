## First findings (2026-09-25, in flight — scripts/stutter-run.mjs, phone tier 390×844 @3, the player sprinting pier → cove → interior, CPU 4×)

- **The near ground-cover refill uploads its whole cap every 4 m: 2.40 MB in 42 `bufferSubData` calls, every ~0.56 s at a
  sprint, even in the Blender cove where 0 of the 20.7 k near instances are live** (the cove is excluded from the cover,
  E156). With only the live count uploaded that frame is 0.01 MB in the cove and ~1 MB in the interior (8.8 k live).
- **The far swap (every 8 m) uploads 2–4 MB at once** (Normal range); with Foliage range 500 m the cover uploaded
  7.9 MB/s in total. Jake's 1.9–2.4 M triangles point at the 500 m range still saved on his phone (being deleted: E156).
- On this Mac neither shows as a spike (Chromium and Playwright WebKit both eat a 4 MB upload in < 1 ms; the upload frames
  and the 2 after them are +0.3–0.7 ms), so the proof on the phone is Jake's meter. What the Mac does show: the byte
  pattern is exactly periodic with travel (4 m / 8 m), the spike count Jake reads (13–21 / 30 s) sits between the near
  refills (~53 / 30 s sprinting) and the far swaps (~27 / 30 s).
- **The game allocates 16–27 MB/s of JS garbage** while running (V8 heap sampling): a GC every ~1–2 s. V8 on the Mac
  collects it without a hitch; JavaScriptCore on the iPhone runs its collections from the run loop, outside the game's
  update / render — which is where Jake's meter books a frame's time as `gpu~`. Being traced to its allocation sites.
- Not it: shader links (0 during the run), texture uploads (0.3–0.6 MB/s, steady), the sun shadow fade (+~2 ms GPU on
  the ~35 % of frames it runs, no spikes), long tasks (none outside the game loop).

## For d0 (E189, the shadow rig — not edited here)

**The shadow pass re-derives the shared depth material's program 23–30 times a frame.** Casters without a
`customDepthMaterial` all render with three's one shared `MeshDepthMaterial`, and `WebGLRenderer.setProgram` treats a
change of `object.receiveShadow` / `isInstancedMesh` / `instanceColor` between two consecutive casters as a program change:
it re-runs `WebGLPrograms.getParameters` + `getProgramCacheKey` (a ~100-field object, arrays, a joined string) and looks the
program up again. On Driftwood's phone tier there are 250 such casters in traversal order: 204 `mesh-shadow-pieces-*`
(shadowChunks.ts, receiveShadow false) interleaved with 41 receiveShadow-true meshes (`Mesh`, `wreck-rocks`) and 5
instanced ones → 18 flag flips per cascade, 23–30 per frame measured (a counter on `customProgramCacheKey`). V8's heap
sampler books ~240 MB / 30 s ≈ **8 MB/s of garbage** to it (getParameters 172 MB, the cache-key joins ~70 MB), plus the
CPU of ~30 getParameters calls in every frame's render. The fix is the rig's: give each flag combination its own
depth material (e.g. `customDepthMaterial` on the shadow pieces), or set the shadow-only pieces' `receiveShadow` to match
their neighbours — the depth program does not use it. Also 2 flips a frame on one `MeshStandardMaterial` shared by 43
meshes with mixed receiveShadow.

## The fix (live 1517004-muhx93k5: commits 665d9d0c + c01d0e6f)

The spikes are GPU-side waits (`gpu~` 78–84 ms), which on the iPhone is where big buffer uploads land: WebKit hands WebGL to
its GPU process, and a frame that queues megabytes has to wait for it. Every one of them was periodic with travel:

| uploader | before (per event) | how often (sprint 7.2 m/s) | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| near cover refill | 2.40 MB in one frame, 42 calls: the whole cap, even in the cove (0 live) | every 4 m ≈ 0.56 s | only the live instances, ≤ 256 KB a frame into a hidden twin mesh, swapped in one frame; ~0 in the cove |
| far cover swap | 2–4 MB in one frame (Normal); 5–11 MB per 20° turn in the deleted 500 m range | every 8 m ≈ 1.1 s | the same slice-a-frame upload into a twin, swapped when done |
| Blender island tile, first sight | 0.3–1.6 MB per tile, several tiles in one frame (up to 2.8 MB), as you ran into range or turned round | each new tile | made one per frame, nearest-first, up to 30 m before it shows (out of view: not culled that frame; hidden: drawn at a range of 0) |

- **GroundCover.ts:** each tier of each kind is a `Tier`: two InstancedMeshes on twin geometries (the model shared). The next
  set is written into the hidden one, only its written instances are marked for upload, 256 KB a frame for the whole
  cover (the near set first), and the two swap in one frame. The near set now starts at 3 m and must swap within 1 m more
  (else the rest goes that frame), so the old rule still holds: the shown set was built at most 4 m away. Both meshes are
  drawn once at boot (count 0) so their GPU buffers are made there, not mid-run. Same plants, same data, same shader.
- **Look check:** popin-fly `--popcheck` (a refill forced at the same pose, near and far) differs between A and B exactly as
  much on HEAD as with the fix (0–2 k pixels of sway noise in both); HEAD vs the fix at the same pose differ only by the
  wind phase (outlines of the same plants, the palms and the clouds). Nothing is scaled, faded or sunk.
- **Garbage:** the cover's cell generation (per-kind number[] → preallocated scratch), coverTint `sample` (5 arrays a call),
  the collider bridge (a Set refilled + an entries array per mirror every fixed step), BakedTerrain `heightAt` (2 tuples
  a call), `waveDisplace` (destructuring loop). Chrome's allocation rate while sprinting: 26.7–27.0 → 24.4–24.5 MB/s. The
  rest is mostly three's shadow-pass program flips (~8 MB/s, d0 is fixing it in the shadow rig, above), Chrome's own
  number boxing at the WebGL / Rapier bindings (JavaScriptCore does not box), and one-time audio renders.

**Before / after** — scripts/stutter-run.mjs, phone tier 390×844 @3, CPU 4×, 2 × 30 s each, the same sprint (pier → cove →

The Mac (Chromium, and Playwright WebKit too) swallows a 5 MB upload without a hitch, so the spike count can't move here —
the proof is Jake's meter on the phone. What the numbers show is that the periodic multi-megabyte frames are gone.
