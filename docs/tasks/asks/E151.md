## What landed (2026-09-25)

The big light / dark triangles came from the sea's per-facet shading: every 4 m (phone) grid triangle was flat-shaded and
graded on its own normal (`water *= 1 + fn.x·4.2 + fn.z·2.6`, ±40 %), so each facet flipped light or dark.

- **Smooth wave normal per pixel.** `waves.ts` gains `WAVES_NORMAL_GLSL`: the analytic Gerstner normal of the same four
  waves the boat rides. The sea takes it at each pixel's rest point, so the lighting (the toon ramp), the grade, the
  fresnel and the glint follow the waves, not the grid. The mesh, its tris and its draw are unchanged.
- **The low-poly look moves into colour.** The grade from the smooth normal is 2.6× the old one (picked from
  1.0 / 1.4 / 1.8 / 2.6 on phone captures), quantised into hard-edged 10 % bands. The swell draws as stylised contour
  bands; the foam, the depth ramp and the E125 rings and horizon fade are untouched.
- **Switchable (taste rule):** `?sea=v1` = the faceted sea as it was; `?sea=soft` = the smooth normal without bands.
- **WebGPU port** (`src/gpu/ocean.ts`) mirrors it (TSL `gerstnerNormal`, `normalNode`, the banded grade). Found on the
  way: the WebGPU sea never drew on the live build. Chrome's Tint (Metal) rejected its vertex stage ("swizzle view
  instruction still has usages after lowering", from `positionLocal.xz`). The rest point is now built as its own
  `vec2`, and the sea draws under `?gpu=webgpu`.
- What is left in the bridge gully is the seabed's own low-poly terrain seen through clear shallow water. That is the
  design, and it is not the sea's shading.

**Phone cost** (tier=phone, 390×844 @3×, M5 Max, timer query around `composer.render`, frames with the sea minus
without it, median of 3 rounds × 100 frames): boat side 1.85 → 1.93 ms, beach 1.27 → 1.36 ms, bridge 1.22 → 1.34 ms.
That is **+0.05–0.12 ms** (~+5–10 % of the sea's own pass) for 4 sin/cos pairs per pixel. **Tris and draws: unchanged**
(the same mesh, one draw).
