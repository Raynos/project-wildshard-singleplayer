## Faces agent (2026-09-30): an honest assessment of the method, and what else could be done

**What the B heads are.** Each is a real Hunyuan3D-2 mesh (a head-and-shoulders bust generated from a codex front
portrait), cut to the neck, scaled so its neck-to-top matches the old head, decimated to ~2.4 k (phone) / 4 k tris and
welded onto the shipped body. Then the same portrait is **projected straight onto the front of that head** (orthographic,
along one axis, faded out by how much a point faces the camera) and everything is baked into one new atlas where the face
gets ~25–35 % instead of ~2 %. So Jake's read is right: the sharp face you see is largely the photo laid over the mesh.

**Where it breaks**
- **Sides and ears.** A front projection has no information past ~45°. The portrait's pixels stretch across the cheeks,
  temples and ears, then cross-fade into Hunyuan's own (softer, differently coloured) paint. From 3/4 and profile there
  is a visible band where the photo ends; ears get smeared hair / skin; the back of the head is Hunyuan's paint alone.
- **Registration.** The portrait is placed by its cutout's bounding box, not by the mesh's features. A few pixels off and
  the painted eyes sit above or below the mesh's eye sockets, or the photo's hat-brim shadow lands on the forehead/eyes
  (the cook's "glasses", a band across Hale's brim — masked with `--project-top`, not solved). A painted eye on a lump
  that isn't an eye reads as a decal the moment the head turns.
- **Lighting baked twice.** The codex portrait already carries studio shading (cheek and nose shadows, a key light, AO,
  specular on the skin). Baked into albedo and then lit again by the game, the face gets double shading, a different
  contrast and colour from the body texture next to it, and it does not respond to the sun — it looks like a photo stuck
  on. Worst on Pine Hollow (photoreal skin next to a generated body texture); milder on Nalati's painterly look.
- **The neck seam.** A horizontal cut at the narrowest section. Hunyuan's thin shirt collars and fur edges tear when
  decimated, so shards sit round the neck (Brandt's collar, Mott's hat rim); an old beard has to be cut away and
  covered by the new one; a high coat collar needs `--body-collar`. The seam is hidden at 2.5 m from the front, not from
  the side or above.
- **Hats and proportions.** Matching neck-to-top makes a new hat bigger or smaller than the old one (Hale's hat shrank;
  the Golden King's tall hat needed its own `--keep-above` path). The head's silhouette changes, not only its face.
- **Resolution.** On the phone the whole figure shares a 512² atlas, so even at 30 % the face is ~150 px wide.

**Alternatives (not built)**
1. **Hunyuan3D-2's own multi-view paint, no portrait projection.** Its texture comes from a multi-view diffusion paint
   of the whole head: consistent all the way round, no decal edge, lit neutrally (Hunyuan's paint runs a delight step).
   Faces come out softer and blotchier than the photo, but they would turn with the head. *Cost:* the busts exist;
   re-bake without `--bust-image` — minutes per head on CPU, no model time. The cheapest A/B, and the first thing to try.
2. **Delit, multi-view projection.** Run Hunyuan3D-2's delight model on the portrait before projecting (flattens the
   baked shadows so the face sits in the game's shading), and project from three views (front + two 3/4 portraits from
   codex) blended by facing, so the sides are real pixels, not stretched ones. *Cost:* ~1 min per image on the model lock
   for the delight; 2 extra codex images per character (parallel, ~3 min); ~a day to write the multi-view blend and
   feature-based registration (eyes / mouth landmarks instead of the alpha box).
3. **The whole figure from one portrait-quality image, no graft.** A full-body A-pose reference with a large, readable
   face → one Hunyuan3D-2 figure, then the face-first re-UV + bake we already have. No neck seam, no hat mismatch, one
   paint. *Cost:* one codex image + ~2–3 min of Hunyuan per character, the rigs re-run (Pine and the camp rig at load; the
   King through rig_transfer); risk: the costume drifts from the approved bodies, and a whole-body generation still gives
   the face few triangles.
4. **A hand-authored Blender head per style.** One clean base head per shard look (toon faceted, painterly, PBR) with a
   proper UV, real eye sockets and lids, per-character beard / hat / skin tone, grafted at the neck as now. Topology that
   can blink and move a jaw later. *Cost:* the highest: ~1–2 days per style for the base, ~2–4 h per character; the most
   controllable result and the one that would hold up close.

My recommendation: try 1 on the Pine and Nalati busts first (hours, nearly free) and put it on the same angle sheets
beside B; if the faces still don't hold up from the side, 4 is the method that would, 2 the cheaper middle.
