# E112

**Status:** done (2026-09-24) — commit c738aba, live build c738aba-mugfdw5m
**Ask:** (from E110/E111) sweep Driftwood's other thin two-sided swaying cloth / leaf meshes for the same self-shadow triangle acne


**Found (2026-09-24, A/B: each mesh with receiveShadow on vs off, desktop 1600×900 + phone tier 390×844, morning and evening sun):**
the cause was wider than cloth. Every `lowPolyMaterial` model is drawn two-sided, and three offsets the shadow lookup
along the *geometric* normal (`normalBias` 0.14). A single sheet wound away from the sun had its lookup pushed through
itself, so its sunlit face read its own shadow:
- **the hut's hipped roof** (Hut.ts `hip()`: all four thatch layers wound inward) sat in shade all day, dark brown
  instead of its golden thatch;
- **the wreck's torn sail** came out dark grey with ragged lit edges on its sunlit (south) side;
- **the lookout banner's back** was in shade in the evening (sun in the west) with a lit strip down one edge.
Clean, no change: the palm fronds (their winding faces the sun), bushes (front-sided volumes), seabed (casts nothing),
rocks / pier / posts (closed shapes).

**Fix:** src/world/lowpolyKit.ts `lowPolyMaterial` flips each face's shadow normal toward the CSM light
(`csm.lightDirection`, so it follows the day clock and the moon) before the bias. One shader line, no extra draw calls,
and the sheets keep *receiving* shadows (unlike the boat-sail split). Faces turned from the light are unlit by N·L
anyway, so closed shapes don't change (checked: a pier / rocks / posts frame live vs local is identical).
