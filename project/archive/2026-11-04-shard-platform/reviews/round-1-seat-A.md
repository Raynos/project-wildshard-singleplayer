1. **Fail.** SF1 cannot establish its expanded inventory under its own shrink-only rule, lacks a portable historical comparison, and bans Thin Ice’s exception (A1–A3). SF4 has a clear library/parity objective, but the F0 pickup as a whole is not executable without resolving these conflicts.
2. **Fail.** SF7 establishes the format-writing task; SF8–SF14 supply the intended minimum systems; SF15 supplies loading. The walk stalls on unspecified M1 content, admission enforcement, state scopes, and complete restore semantics before SF16 can prove the boundary (A5–A11).
3. **Fail.** F1 supplies material families, entity scripts, quest/dialogue data, one brain/spawner, and a local ledger. Those are the right minimum ingredients. Door state-to-visual/collision bindings, the required author trial, and reward semantics remain undefined; the later UI/director/rig catalogue is an expected expansion, but the basic battery shard must work at M1 (A5, A7, A10–A11, A14).
4. **Fail.** SF17 builds the seam, SF18 streams, SF19 supplies one frame, and SF20 replaces reload travel. During the three-second stall the car advances 90 m; no selected payload/ring caps or collision-readiness/fallback contract guarantees the next seconds of travel. Wallet continuity also lacks an acceptance test (A11–A14).
5. **Fail.** SF46 orders compression/sharing before crossroads admission; SF9 supplies tiling and SF18 aggregate residency. However, no selected dependency-closed target determines when Pine Hollow is ready, and its 587 MB GPU ratchet cannot answer total phone residency (A12).
6. **Fail.** The future team can rely on the intended renderer-neutral package, bounded script ABI, headless execution, and ledger seam. The plan does not yet guarantee complete restored state, actor-scoped authority, render-independent simulation, or compatibility of each converted shard; 80/20 can still leave gameplay outside the package (A6–A9, A14–A16).
7. **Fail.** Settings ▸ Developer is an existing surface for Signal Dunes/Sky Reach membership, and G46 specifies isolated explore. Nine Dragon’s DEVSERVER entry point, mode transitions, and safe removal of an occupied dev shard remain unspecified (A17).
8. **Pass.** SF19 gives the replacement contract: materials plus per-pixel grade under one camera-owned frame. SF47 explicitly checks Nalati against it; §9 retains the old look as a Debug variant and sends a portrait board for Jake’s pick. Its current replacement composer is confirmed by `src/shards/nalati-grasslands/look/render.ts:19–25`.
9. **Fail.** SF11 stops the built-path endless loop and memory hoarder. It does not yet guarantee refusal of substituted unmetered Wasm, aggregate host-work containment, atomic handling of NaN/trapped output, or a defined playable recovery state. SF14’s duplicate-fact test also needs durable provenance and reload coverage (A8–A11).
10. **Fail.** SF7 records the targeted API and SF43 offers migration/playtests. There is no specified v0 runtime support during the window, retirement behavior afterward, or ownership of the three upgrades; the current equality check would reject v0 immediately after a bump (A16).

The central sequence is sound: thin M1 tests the package and authoring boundary early; the singleplayer crossroads then tests the streaming and visual envelope before format freeze; conversions and later systems expose real SDK gaps. No networking implementation is needed to establish those contracts. The weakness is that the current exits prove a smaller result than the goal claims, particularly where template-only parity, cold replay, or an 80/20 migration percentage substitutes for portable gameplay.

Verdict: Keep thin M1 → the singleplayer grid → the lanes, but resolve the contradictory F0 gates and specify the admission, state, streaming, versioning, and per-shard compatibility exits before marking this execution plan ready.
