**Driving play:** spawn and choose the initial yaw/pitch before frame zero; then release movement/aim/attack events at recorded sim times. Keep the normal camera, collision, animal AI, weapon state and consequences; no camera spline, per-frame teleport, CALM, hidden enemy geometry or staged victory. Record an approach/aim/fire/recovery, not only a trigger flash.
**Day 1:** load its own build with its existing `skipintro=1&nolock=1&mute=1` (see `mkshots.py:34`), use `__world`, disable tour, keep `crossbow.enabled/model.visible`. `nolock` supplies `allowUnlocked`; movement uses `player.keys`, look uses yaw/pitch, ADS uses `crossbow.adsHeld`, and trigger uses `tryFire()` (`568a1463f:Crossbow.ts:534–565`). For a five-second take: W 0–1 s, release; settle/aim 1–2 s; ADS 2–2.5 s; fire at 2.5 s; reaction/recovery through 5 s. Find real nearby prey first; reject absence/miss if the edit promises impact.
**Legacy fixed step:** extend the historical capture spec/adapter, not the historical game: after boot/warm-up, pause Playwright's fake clock, take control of queued RAF callbacks, and override THREE `clock.getDelta()` to advance `elapsedTime` by exactly 1/60 per game frame. Pump one game-loop callback, advance fake wall time on the same timeline, then screenshot; assert frame-count delta = 1. Day 1 lacks `frameGate`, so a fake-clock `runFor(33)` alone is not this guarantee. Day 8 can additionally use its existing gate. This adapter is work to build/prove, not a claim that v1 already does it.
**Day 8:** same legacy clock/global adapter; use its Weapons manager, `player.keys` and yaw/pitch; retain normal player movement. **Day 15/22:** use `__wildshard.world`, its existing App clock/gate, fixed 60 fps with `SUB=1`; inspect whether that revision consumes keys or InputService before selecting the driver. **HEAD:** use InputService held actions/presses (`InputService.ts:150,175`), yaw/pitch as look input, then normal stepping; current `touchJump` still funnels into `press('jump')` (`Player.ts:413–415`). No added URL variants at HEAD; use harness/settings fixtures for Developer admission and capture setup.
**Reuse boundary:** the trailer owner adds external progress shot/cut config support and the version adapter seam. Progress keeps its manifest/configs and prepared authoring videos; shared titles, mix and edit run unchanged. Historical frames can be packaged as finished clips through `edl.clips[].video` (`edit.mjs:47–52`), avoiding a second editing pipeline. Existing v1 capture is the historical bridge, not the final free-camera gameplay recipe.
**Build/record procedure:** export each exact SHA with `build-rev.sh` in owned scratch; use its lockfile and the heavy build lane, not a whole-folder HEAD dependency link across old revisions. Pin HEAD separately through the normal clean build/link process; serve static builds and check served identity before shooting. Wrap all capture in browser-lane, muted; no dev server. Save SHA, aspect, recipe hash, inputs, sim start/end, prey/action result and first/middle/last proofs. Review phone proofs as iPhone 16 Pro portrait; these offline trailer renders are not phone performance evidence.
**Authoring procedure:** `shard-progress.mjs --sha=… --url=… --root=<scratch>` records cameras/metadata, not build creation or SHA verification (`:35,43,163,210`); pair it with the actual served build. `shard-timelapse.py` supplies archive ordering/caption conventions, but curate originals into 5 s beats rather than squeeze every capture. PDFs/mockup boards can guide stage selection, never stand in for rendered historical progress. Later archives/HEAD need new captures if the end card claims their latest state.
**Week 4:** append a date/SHA chapter manifest, one new play take and one selected authoring progression; keep earlier captures cached by SHA/recipe/aspect. Re-run titles, score edit, mix and conform for each aspect; re-film older shots only if a framing/driver fix changes their recipe.
**Sound:** reuse a licensed MiniMax cue from the shared pipeline; place impact at the first crossbow shot and the largest rise at 25–35 s. Add matched crossbow/footstep/bridge/grapple cues from existing approved assets; generate missing SFX through both mandated engines, select one, and mix to −14 LUFS/−1 dBTP with encoded-file verification. Muted capture supplies pictures, not an audio recording. Carry required music/SFX credits.
**Rewind moment (S9):** at 25–35 s, the triangular Sky island grows into the windmill; on the hit, its finished camera becomes the player's eye and the player walks onto the bridge. Keep the hand/fan and footsteps across the cut so authored world becomes playable world.
**Two biggest risks:** (1) version/clock/input drift makes plausible pictures with incorrect time or no real movement—prove five seconds per revision before full capture; (2) terminal source access and changing archive cameras make a false or unreadable authoring story—obtain TR5 assets, curate originals, and label dates/demo honestly. The known grid-loading failure stays outside this cut and with TR9.

Verdict: Replace v1 with an alternating, genuinely input-driven progress story, and prove the historical capture adapters before committing to the full shoot.
